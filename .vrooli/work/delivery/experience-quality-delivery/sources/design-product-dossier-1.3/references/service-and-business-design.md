# Service and business design coverage

Use when the requested design includes an offer, customer acquisition, human-delivered service, commercial model or operating business. Select relevant questions; do not require a software UI, legal opinion or invented financial forecast. In a hybrid, link the service promises to product/system capabilities and evidence.

## Define the customer and offer

- Identify the customer, payer, beneficiary and operator; separate different buying journeys and evidence chains
- Describe the buying situation, job/value, alternatives including doing nothing, fit/disqualification and constraints
- Propose one concrete worked offer when terms are unresolved: outcome, inclusions, limits, exclusions, customer inputs, operator obligations, timing/dependencies, revision/exception allowances and exit. Label every hypothetical price or entitlement as proposed
- Separate one-time setup, initial care/review and recurring value. Show when a recurring obligation starts and what explicitly agreed service sustains it. Do not imply subscription, payment or delivery authorization
- State claim strength and proof: actual observation, external benchmark, synthetic specimen, assumption or evidence still needed. A persuasive proof packet is not a customer result

## Trace the service across the boundary

Create a readable service blueprint or equivalent connected view of acquisition/reach → qualification → agreed scope/payment → onboarding → fulfillment → review/renewal → exit, where applicable. Show customer-facing steps, backstage work, responsible party, inputs/outputs, handoffs, evidence and failure/exception routes. Distinguish interest, purchase, readiness, exact-version approval, dispatch authority, completion and outcome rather than merging them into one status.

Trace important promises to the product, manual process or external provider that would fulfill them. Identify missing readiness evidence, fallback owner, capacity and customer consequence. Show partial delivery, nonresponse, failed payment, uncertain provider outcomes, change requests, weak results and cancellation/exit as relevant. Never retry an uncertain external action blindly or invent a refund/response commitment to fill a gap.

Use domain-appropriate specimens: offer card, proof packet, proposal, customer/operator handoff and service report. Put complete necessary terms somewhere reachable; a neat offer card must not hide obligations or replace full source detail. Preserve approved writing/brand direction and the distinction between private drafts and messages authorized to send.

## Make the economics falsifiable

Connect the offer to a worked model with visible units, period, inputs and provenance. Separate revenue, pass-through spend, cash cost, owner/operator time, contribution and capacity. Count unsuccessful prospects and pre-sale proof effort in acquisition cost; keep reusable creation, marginal customization, setup and recurring work from being omitted or double-counted. Use observed and hypothetical inputs distinctly, never present assumed conversion as measured demand.

Show zero-sales, one-sale and plausible workload/retention cases where material. With no paid customers, preserve unrecovered acquisition cost and undefined cost per acquired customer. Reconcile first-period service obligations and activation timing with available hours. Challenge whether recurring revenue actually covers recurring entitlement rather than treating retention as a cure for an uneconomic offer. Browse current primary sources before relying on external prices, rules or benchmarks.

Define bounded validation experiments with the hypothesis, smallest ethical observation, time/cost cap, success/failure criteria and next decision. Distinguish a correct spreadsheet, owner review and real market or delivery evidence. Do not send outreach, charge, subscribe, launch or change live operations as part of dossier validation without separate authorization.

## Acceptance evidence

A skeptical reader must be able to trace one worked offer from promise to responsibility, capability, cost/capacity, proof, unresolved choice and exit. The service blueprint must expose who handles the difficult handoff and what happens when delivery evidence is missing. Reconcile terms across the offer, specimens, economics and first-outcome route. Index consequential commercial choices without creating a competing project queue. Record what cannot be established without actual customer or operating evidence.
