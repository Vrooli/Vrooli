# Design a reviewable dossier

Produce a document that lets an owner understand the proposed product, service or system, make consequential decisions efficiently, and give future delivery or implementation work an unambiguous design basis. Match depth to complexity and the user's requested scope. Do not optimize for page count, checklist completion, or superficial polish.

If a linked reference cannot be read through skill lookup, read the matching relative file from this skill's verified owned checkout or an explicitly adopted complete project-local copy. Verify source identity and revision; distinguish that source access from a successful skill-tool load. Do not silently skip a required reference, substitute a partial presentation guide for the complete design method, or upgrade a project's adoption. If no authorized complete source is accessible, state the affected design or review gap. An access denial is not permission to try another route.

## Establish authority and boundaries

1. Read the project's own entry point, authoritative specifications, decision records, visual references, and local workflow instructions. Reconcile conflicts using its authority rules. Preserve owner edits and historical outcomes.
2. Identify the specific requested design, audience, artifact destination, review scope, permissions, and exclusions. State whether the current assignment covers a whole product/service/system, chapter, flow, or final artifact; do not fault a scoped chapter for unrelated missing areas. Separate adjacent products and historical names unless identity is evidenced.
3. Distinguish owner-approved choices, source-stated but unverified claims, current implementation observations, assistant recommendations, and unresolved questions. Attach dates and source locators where they matter. An attractive mockup is not approval or proof of implementation.
4. Follow project-owned organization and identifiers. Adapt this method locally; do not require a shared schema, central registry, or live skill dependency. A project may pin or copy a revision and evolve it independently. A shared update does not silently upgrade a project-local method, change settled design or resume paused work.

## Plan substantive coverage

Read [coverage-and-traceability](references/coverage-and-traceability.md). Select a product/UX, service/business, systems/infrastructure, or declared hybrid coverage profile from the actual brief. Read [service and business design](references/service-and-business-design.md) for commercial/service scope and [systems and infrastructure design](references/systems-and-infrastructure-design.md) for system scope. Load only relevant profiles; justify material exclusions rather than forcing screens, commerce or architecture into every dossier. Build an evidence inventory and coverage map before drafting. Identify what already exists and what new design work is needed. Do not pass off concatenation or reformatting of a long historical specification as completed design exploration.

Propose a reader-oriented chapter structure and a realistic work sequence. When the user requests an extensive dossier, cover the requested design coherently, including difficult states and tradeoffs. Explicitly record exclusions and blockers. Keep decisions resumable without needing to read every page.

## Design and author

Read [document-and-visual-craft](references/document-and-visual-craft.md). Use the appropriate document, diagram, image, and artifact tools and their native instructions. Produce editable source plus the requested review format. Preserve native document identity for revisions when appropriate.

For a substantial visual product/UX dossier, include a separate mockup atlas by default, following the visual-atlas contract in that reference. Give the owner two clear entry modes: browse full-size visuals, or read the complete design/specification. Respect explicit scope and project-local adoption; a scoped text repair or nonvisual service/system design does not require an atlas or decorative screens.

For review-surface composition, use the project's canonical pointer and adopted revision to load the shared Craft review artifacts capability and its Native review surfaces reference. Use a labeled self-contained snapshot with provenance only when independent or offline handoff needs it; do not require a whole-guide copy in every project. Test the entry and chapter reading paths on the exact native deliverable. Keep the proposal, decision consequence and next owner action visible together without losing source/uncertainty distinctions. Use its PDF production reference only for a requested PDF or PDF-specific repair; a résumé-specific export requirement does not set the dossier format. Respect project-local adoption and preserve existing source, review and application mechanisms.

For product mockups, read [mockup craft and lifecycle](references/mockup-craft-and-lifecycle.md). Anchor the visual standard in inspected reference pixels, validate one representative screen/state exemplar before scaling, with paired viewports when the project convention or request warrants them, and preserve each version's durable artifacts, exact generation inputs and independently queryable lifecycle dimensions while authoring. Adapt these concepts to the project's own records; do not create a competing decision queue or shared registry dependency.

Work through the requested design under clearly labeled assumptions where approval is unavailable. Make recommendations instead of stopping at every uncertainty. Queue consequential choices for the owner; group independent minor defaults. Do not convert proposed choices into settled facts.

Use domain-appropriate diagrams and specimens to resolve relationships, behavior, service promises and presentation decisions. Use mockups when an interface is part of the design. Compare meaningful alternatives under the same scenario and, for screens, viewport. Reuse approved assets with provenance; create missing coverage where needed. Inspect every delivered page and visual at readable scale.

## Challenge, revise, and validate

Read [critical-review](references/critical-review.md). Define artifact-specific failure modes and acceptance evidence before review. Conduct distinct substantive critique passes, including an independent reviewer when available, on the actual artifact and its sources. Address serious findings and rerun the affected journeys and dependent checks.

Apply the complete decision-index and first-outcome-versus-future-breadth gates in coverage-and-traceability. Check the reader contract on the exact delivered artifact, not only on its outline. A functional check does not establish usability or visual quality. A reviewer agreeing with the author does not establish owner acceptance. If a time or work boundary is reached with material unresolved issues, deliver an explicitly incomplete review candidate with those issues, not a success claim.

## Deliver and improve the local workflow

Deliver the actual reviewed version, its direct location, a concise change summary, approval questions, coverage limits, and a documented critique/revision record. Verify links, images, navigation, exports, and source attribution in the delivery destination. Keep the current recommendation easy to find without hiding uncertainty.

Identify the actual delivery set in the existing project record. When revising, reconcile every affected member: the dossier, the applicable visual atlas or other visual collection, original/editable assets, requested exports, version/catalog records and entry links. Verify that current views resolve to the intended source and visual versions, with matching content and exact checked identities. Inspect each promised review surface at its intended reading size; a readable companion does not excuse unreadable decision details in the main document. Preserve unchanged artifacts and historical versions with their scope clear; report any stale or unverified member as a remaining delivery limit. The visual-atlas default does not require an additional PDF or every possible export format.

Record the application handoff in the project's existing mechanism: exact artifact/revision and delivery/review state; accepted, rejected and deferred scope with approval evidence; affected source targets; authorized integration status; and recheck evidence or the explicit gap. Keep owner approval → authorized source application → verified application → separate implementation/runtime evidence distinct. An unreviewed recommendation remains unapproved and unapplied. Dossier delivery does not grant new permissions, resume paused work or dispatch implementation.

Record useful friction and proposed process improvements in the project's existing mechanism. Separate observations from suspected causes. Explain the smallest immediate workaround and any justified durable change. Test improvements on a subsequent artifact before calling the method mature.

For provenance and optional project-local adoption, read [sources-and-adoption](references/sources-and-adoption.md).
