# Revenue Line: Lead Generation (for local service businesses)

> Offer Desk is authoritative for this revenue line's current status, owner,
> and activation record. This document retains the hypothesis, legal surface,
> and productization judgment rather than a live line snapshot.

- **Revisit trigger:** *"Revisit when at least one property-services scenario is deployable as a thin tool AND one local-service prospect signs a pilot agreement."*
- **Productization target:** [`property-services` add-on](../catalog/addons/property-services.md)
- **Legal surface:** TCPA (US telemarketing), CAN-SPAM (email), GDPR (if international), state-level rules on B2B vs B2C lead sales. Non-trivial; requires explicit legal review before first paid engagement.

## Hypothesis

Local service businesses (power washing, landscaping, flippers, solar installers, roofers) will pay per-lead or per-close for geo-targeted leads generated by Vrooli's data + AI capabilities. If it works for us, we can productize the underlying tool into the `property-services` add-on.

## Why this is the strongest candidate services line

1. **High capability reuse** — the `property-services` add-on already scopes most of the underlying tooling (geo scanning, visualization, estimating). Running lead-gen internally builds the same tools we'd productize.
2. **Immediate revenue is realistic** — per-lead or per-close pricing is well-established in this market; prospects understand it without education.
3. **Clean conversion path** — "we built you a tool that generates these leads; now subscribe to the add-on and run it yourself" is a concrete, linear pitch. Conversion doesn't require re-framing the relationship.

## Candidate vertical playbooks

Captured at walk #5 (2026-04-28) from operator-surfaced external content (specifically Post 1 — *"ChatGPT 5.5 found my first clients on Google Maps in 10 minutes"* — and Post 2 — *MapiLeads B2B prospecting tool description*). All entries below are tagged `unverified-third-party-claim`: the workflows are structural patterns worth knowing about; the quantitative claims are upper-bound aspirational and require independent measurement before landing in any pricing or sizing decision. See `docs/marketing/strategy/STRATEGY.md`'s "Source material discipline" section for the discipline applied here.

These are *candidate playbooks for activation phase*, not commitments. None of them is `active`. When this revenue line activates, one vertical + one playbook is selected; the others remain candidate.

### Playbook A — Local-business website-building service

**Source pattern:** target local service businesses (HVAC, plumbing, electrical, pest control, roofing) with strong Google Maps ratings but weak or missing websites; deliver a complete AI-built website in under an hour using AI website-builders (e.g., Framer / Webflow / Wix-with-AI / Lovable) seeded by an AI-authored brief.

**Structural mechanics worth borrowing:**
- Lead-source = Google Maps (search by business type + city; filter by rating + website-presence).
- Brief generation = LLM prompted with business name, services from Maps profile, and best customer reviews → produces a website-builder brief with hero / services / testimonials / about-us / CTA sections.
- Production = AI website-builder consumes the brief → live preview in <1 hour.
- Pitch = show the live preview; conversion happens because the prospect can see the result, not because they're buying a promise.

**Pricing tiers observed in source (`unverified-third-party-claim`):** basic site $500-800; full site with booking $1,000-1,500; site + monthly maintenance $1,200 upfront + $200/month.

**Operational targets to measure if activated:** prospect-to-preview time; preview-to-close rate; client churn at 3 months; per-client time investment.

**Legal surface:** standard commercial contract terms; ownership of generated assets; hosting/domain handling. Less regulated than direct outreach (covered in Playbook C).

### Playbook B — Reputation management

**Source pattern:** every Google Maps business under 4.2 stars is losing customers; service = monitor reviews, respond using LLM-drafted templates, and run review-request flows to lift the score.

**Structural mechanics worth borrowing:**
- Daily morning sweep across all client review surfaces (Google Maps, Yelp, etc.).
- LLM-drafted responses with operator approval per response (or auto-publish for clearly-positive reviews; manual review for ambiguous/negative).
- Review-request automation (post-service email/SMS to customers).
- Recurring monthly retainer pricing.

**Pricing tier observed in source (`unverified-third-party-claim`):** $300-800/month/client; reported time investment 20-30 minutes/morning across 5 clients.

**Operational targets to measure if activated:** rating-lift over 90 days; per-client time investment; churn at 6 months.

**Legal surface:** TOS compliance for each platform's review-response and review-request rules (Google has explicit policies on review solicitation); FTC endorsement guidelines if any incentive is offered.

### Playbook C — Cold outreach automation

**Source pattern:** scrape lead lists from Maps using third-party scrapers (Outscraper, PhantomBuster, MapiLeads-class tools); LLM qualifies leads against criteria; LLM personalizes cold emails referencing each lead's specific Maps data (rating, review count, missing-website signal).

**Structural mechanics worth borrowing:**
- Three-stage pipeline: scrape → qualify → personalize.
- Personalization signal = lead's specific public data, not generic templating; the email reads as if a human looked at their business specifically.
- Qualification filters: rating range (e.g., 3.5-4.2 = "underperforming but legitimate"), review count threshold, website-presence check.

**Volume claims observed in source (`unverified-third-party-claim`):** 500 emails/day at 3% reply rate → ~15 leads/day → 1-2 closes/day at 10% close rate. **All four numbers are upper-bound aspirational. Treat as hypothesis-generators, not benchmarks.**

**Operational targets to measure if activated:** deliverability rate; reply rate; lead-to-meeting rate; meeting-to-close rate; cost per close.

**Legal surface:** **highest among the three playbooks.** TCPA (US telemarketing — applies to SMS/calls; emails covered separately), CAN-SPAM (email, US), GDPR (international), CASL (Canada), state-level B2B-vs-B2C distinctions. Cold outreach at scale is heavily regulated and varies by jurisdiction. Activation requires explicit legal review — non-trivial.

### Reference architecture: comprehensive prospecting tool

**Source pattern:** Post 2 (MapiLeads) describes a single-tool architecture combining Maps scraping, multi-data-point extraction (30+ fields including emails, phones, social, hours, GPS), AI review-mining for pain-point detection, offer-cross-referencing for personalized outreach, single-shot send (not bulk), CRM with GPS-mapped sales zones, route optimization, and voice-note transcription.

**Why this matters for productization:** the `property-services` add-on's ultimate productization target may benefit from this architecture's shape — one tool that owns the whole prospecting → outreach → meeting → tracking pipeline rather than separate tools at each stage. Captured here as a reference architecture for when productization design starts; not a commitment to mirror it.

**Worth investigating during activation:** what specifically the audience values vs. what's marketing flourish; whether the single-tool integration is the differentiator vs. the AI-personalization step alone.

## Notes

- Legal surface check is the single biggest gate before activation. Do not skip.
- Start with one vertical, not a basket. Vertical-specific pricing and regulatory context differ sharply.
- The candidate playbooks above are starting points for vertical-pilot design, not playbooks to be executed verbatim. Adapt to the chosen vertical and validate every borrowed number against measured Vrooli data before publishing or pricing against it.
