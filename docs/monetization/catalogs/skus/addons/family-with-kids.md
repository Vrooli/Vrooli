> relationship, and revisit-trigger record. This document retains the

**SKU ID:** `family-with-kids`
**Revisit trigger:** *"Revisit when the lifestyle bundle is `active` AND ≥3 distinct prospects explicitly request child/family-oriented tooling."*

## Hypothesis

Households with children have recurring operational needs that benefit from a shared, local-first coordination layer — especially when the family runs Vrooli on local hardware and gives kids controlled access to age-appropriate agents.

Candidate capabilities:

- **Chore tracking with rewards** — assign chores, verify completion, grant rewards (device time, credits, allowance)
- **Homework help and tutoring** — per-subject tutor agents with parent-visible logs
- **Screen time and content management** — local enforcement rather than cloud-account-based
- **Kids' games that reward real-world tasks** — gamification of chores, reading, learning
- **Family calendar and logistics** — shared schedules, carpool coordination, permission slips
- **Age-appropriate AI access** — kids interact with safety-filtered agents, parents see summaries

## Structural advantage

This add-on is a particularly strong fit for **Tier 3 (hosted) and Tier 4 (hardware appliance)** because:

- Local-first ownership matters for children's data more than almost any other category
- Hardware appliances in the home make kids' access to compute a natural shared-family resource
- Parents distrust cloud SaaS with kids' data

When hosted or hardware tiers activate, this add-on's appeal increases disproportionately.

## Promotion constraints

1. The lifestyle bundle must be active before this add-on can be promoted.
2. The "kids-facing AI" category has significant trust, safety, and regulatory requirements (COPPA in the US; strong parent expectations about content filtering and data retention). The Vrooli runtime does not yet have the guardrails infrastructure this demands.
3. Many of the headline features (rewards, games, tutors) are specific applications that don't reuse as much existing capability as they might appear to — they are real new work.

## Signals to monitor before promotion

- Lifestyle bundle activation
- Maturity of content-filtering and age-appropriate-response capability in the Vrooli agent stack
- Prospect signal (requests from actual parents, not just the operator's enthusiasm)
- Tier 3 / Tier 4 progress — this add-on's appeal is amplified by those tiers

## If and when promoted

First-pass questions at activation:

1. Which capability is the single strongest acquisition hook? (Chore rewards has broad appeal; tutoring has deeper value but narrower audience.)
2. Age bands — the product for a 5-year-old is totally different from one for a 13-year-old. Start narrow.
3. What's the parent-side dashboard vs. the kid-side interface split?
4. What safety and content-filtering guarantees do we make, and how do we validate them?
