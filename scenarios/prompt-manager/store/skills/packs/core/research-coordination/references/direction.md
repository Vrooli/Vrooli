# Research direction and handoff

Read when commissioning sustained research, changing its direction, or preparing a successor. For a short question, put the relevant information in the prompt or existing brief. No additional files or supervisor are required.

## Choose artifacts and startup context

Do not copy another topic's folder layout by default. Read the operator's objective, current topic guide and latest feedback, then use the fewest artifacts that give the next investigator a reliable entry path.

| Work shape | Primary representation | Add another artifact only when it has a distinct job |
|---|---|---|
| One sustained direction | Commission with a concise direction brief | Add a checkpoint for changed findings and live effects; do not duplicate the direction in a second file. |
| Several dependent decisions or branches | Commission plus an unranked decision map | A map may carry the current direction itself. Do not select a lead unless the operator selected it or evidence supports that priority. |
| Feedback-sensitive artifacts or evaluations | Record feedback against the exact version and aspect reviewed | Add a ledger when versions/aspects need ongoing comparison; keep accepted and unresolved judgments distinct. |
| Research with later approval or live proof | Separate the research outcome from the later gate and name its return trigger | Do not make deferred implementation or live proof a prerequisite for finishing authorized research. |

For a broad, open question, form an initial framing from the mission and current evidence before reviewing a curator's candidate list or ranked tool output. Then use maps and suggestions to check alternatives and dependencies. Do not require an idea quota, brainstorming stage or fixed sequence. Mark material directions as operator-selected, evidence-selected or curator-suggested. Preserve tested-and-rejected ideas separately from untested or deferred ones.

Name one first-read path in the topic or run guide. The first read must carry the mission, actual authority and current status. Point from it to one relevant map/brief and a short checkpoint when they change the next action. Keep source maps and historical evidence available for targeted lookup; do not require every linked file at startup. Reuse one file for several roles when that does not hide a real boundary.

When fresh external evidence materially affects a study, read `web-search` and
use its owner research program. Check `vrooli scenario status web-search` before
the first live call; if the scenario is stopped, start it through
`make -C scenarios/web-search start` and verify health. Search Hub's leaf status
is separate: an open circuit or stale evaluation does not prove the direct
Web Search owner is unavailable. If startup or the owner call fails, preserve
the exact failure, try a materially different authorized source route, and
limit any unresolved evidence gap to the dependent claims or study. Keep
independent studies moving. At the first checkpoint, record the skill IDs
actually read, route used, and evidence provenance.

### Prepare without commissioning

When the operator asks to prepare a research folder but not start the research, prepare a reviewable handoff only. Do not create an active goal, start a worker, start a clock, acquire models/data or perform the proposed experiments. Record the proposed allowance separately from approval; leave actual start and deadline unset. Do not list a future approval as an active blocker or send an attention request unless the operator must make a specific decision now. State the exact action that will commission the work. A preparation task does not count as research progress or consume the proposed allowance.

## Commission and working brief

Keep the operator's intent, quality standard, allowed effects, implementation policy, cumulative allowance and stopping conditions in the commission. A research method does not grant authority or impose a new implementation gate. Architecture design, source inquiry, experiments, authorized construction and approval-gated implementation can each change methods as evidence warrants.

Use the commission as the first read for a short task. For sustained work, keep one current direction artifact: the commission itself, a working brief, or a decision map. Create a separate brief only when it carries rationale, evidence, alternatives or investigator freedom that the commission does not already carry. Name the artifact in the run guide. Do not create one for every check or require a whole topic to share one lead. Include the following information where it changes the investigator's decisions:

| Information | What the investigator needs |
|---|---|
| Purpose and stakes | The outcome the operator values and why this direction could advance it. Name whether the intended result is an improved artifact/capability, an explanation, a decision, or a reusable method. Do not require all of them. Preserve consequential user corrections. |
| Current direction | The question or insight to develop, its mechanism, relevant alternatives, and why it deserves attention now. Identify whether the operator selected it, evidence selected it, or a curator merely suggested it. A lead is a hypothesis, not an instruction to confirm it. |
| Evidence and uncertainty | Supported observations, counterevidence, prior failed approaches and exact source pointers. Distinguish rejected ideas from untested or deferred ones. |
| Starting points and freedom | Specific sources, examples or initial checks that avoid rediscovery. State which assumptions, methods and follow-ups the investigator may revise. |
| Authority and continuity | Link the commission and carry forward every applicable effect boundary, implementation gate, exclusion, real stop condition, remaining allowance and unresolved effect owner. Unknown usage stays unknown. |
| Useful outcome and exit | What would justify adoption, deeper study, refutation, transfer or closure. An unknown answer is allowed; unsupported certainty is not. |
| Reading map | Point to the current checkpoint and the evidence sections needed for the next decision. Link deeper background for selective use. |

When the commission leaves direction open, let a decision map or well-chosen evidence support investigator selection. Do not nominate a winner merely to fill a brief. An investigator can propose a direction without waiting for a separate selection agent. When a direction is commissioned, explain its stakes and preserve permission to challenge its assumptions within scope.

Before dispatch, check the brief against the actual commission and latest feedback. A summary cannot override its sources. Resolve contradictions before dependent effects. For a fresh recipient, link any omitted commission detail that could change execution. Read those details before acting. Do not send an unexplained list of folders or require the entire history as startup context.

Keep the checkpoint for changes, current finding, next decision, live effects and cumulative allowance. Keep rationale in the working brief and raw evidence at its existing location. Update on material changes; do not rewrite every view after each tool call. Mark the brief's revision/date in handoffs so feedback reaches the actual recipient. Amend frozen trial protocols prospectively; a broader research reframe does not rewrite past results.

For research with dependent decisions, use the existing topic or brief to map each consequential decision to its evidence, unresolved dependency and next discriminator. Distinguish missing knowledge, an actual authorization gate and a later capability proof. Give deferred work a changed-evidence trigger rather than repeated status checks. Select a focused question for each investigator from this map; do not impose this structure on a simple source question or free construction task.

Match completion to the authorized outcome. Research can finish with a supported architecture or reviewable implementation decision while a human gate remains closed. Label the capability unimplemented or unqualified; do not make a live proof a prerequisite for completing research-only work. When implementation is commissioned, a decision package alone is insufficient. A useful synthesis can collapse several older findings into one decision without another experiment. Preserve the contrary evidence and superseding corrections so successors do not reopen resolved questions from early paragraphs of a chronological report.

For preference-sensitive work, bind a judgment to the exact artifact/version, aspect reviewed, evaluator and review method. Keep accepted aspects visible beside unresolved ones. Technical validity, operator preference, audience effect and publication eligibility can have different verdicts. A component preference does not approve a whole artifact, and an old whole-artifact rating does not rate a later revision. Read relevant cross-topic feedback before treating an old summary as current. A later task-specific instruction can supersede a prerequisite for that task without erasing the broader finding or granting new future effects.

A local repair may be the best answer to an artifact-improvement commission. If the user instead commissioned a reusable research method, treat that repair as a case or lead, not the entire outcome. State what the method could reveal beyond the local result and what evidence would support transfer. Use the smallest perceptual or empirical comparison that preserves the context needed to judge the intended difference; do not force every creative study into a causal experiment or an automatic quality score.

## Develop and reconsider directions

The investigator owns local method selection and successive permitted checks. Pursue implications while they can advance the mission within the remaining allowance. A useful direction may span sessions and contain several experiments or implementation steps. A completed check is a checkpoint. A required approval or exhausted allowance remains a real boundary.

When choosing a broad direction, or when weak returns, surprise, repeated friction or a failed assumption challenge it, reconsider how the problem is framed. Draw on the operator's objective, concrete examples, adjacent disciplines, contrary evidence and capabilities outside the current tool's ranking. Ask what structure or causal mechanism could explain several observations, and what evidence could distinguish it. Do not require a fixed number of ideas, a brainstorming interval, or novelty for its own sake. For a precise question with a strong route, investigate directly.

Compare plausible directions by their potential value, uncertainty they could resolve, available evidence, cost to investigate and consequences if they succeed. An inexpensive initial probe can justify a larger study. Lack of a complete evaluator at the idea stage is a reason to test feasibility, not automatic rejection. Freeze a suitable evaluator before comparative or confirmatory claims. Preserve promising alternatives without developing each into a plan. Judge actual usefulness; screens, deleted lines, passing checks and artifact counts are supporting measures, not universal research outcomes.

Use the first relevant intervention at a material review:

| Observation | Decision |
|---|---|
| Useful findings are accumulating along a coherent direction | Let the investigator deepen it. Add context or a challenge only when it could change the result. |
| A finding changes the likely value of this or another direction | Inspect decisive evidence and revise the direction or shared priorities. |
| Several checks are accurate but add little understanding or capability | Revisit the objective and framing. Seek a different mechanism or perspective before ordering more adjacent checks. |
| One route failed | Separate a failed method from a defeated hypothesis. Choose an authorized repair, alternate method, or another valuable direction. |
| The direction needs more work than fits one context window | Checkpoint and prepare a successor when needed. Preserve the larger question and original allowance. |
| An actual authorization gate blocks one branch | Stop its dependent effects. Continue independent useful permitted research. Retain the decision needed and the trigger for returning to the branch. |
| The commissioned outcome is answered, a real stop condition fires or no useful permitted work remains | Close or hand off with the evidence and exact remaining condition. Do not manufacture activity. |

The supervisor supplies missing context, challenges reasoning and allocates attention across directions. It does not approve every local check. Operational monitoring supplies execution evidence; review the intellectual contribution separately. Use an existing owner wait or notification for pending events instead of repeatedly collecting unchanged status.

## Context and model cost

Keep an active, productive investigator while its relevant context remains useful. Do not replace it after each experiment. At a handoff or context reset, send the curated brief and checkpoint rather than forking an accumulated history by default. Retain a history-preserving fork only when its needed context is cheaper than reconstruction; verify remaining authority and effects either way.

Resolve the current research role through the launch owner. Choose the least costly role that can perform the required judgment. A read-only task can still require difficult conceptual synthesis; reserve cheap lookup roles for well-specified retrieval and bounded checks. When evidence shows shallow reasoning or repeated rework, reconsider the direction, context and role before scaling more of the same. Record requested versus observed settings. Compare total investigation cost, including curation, supervision and recovery, against useful outcomes; input bytes alone do not prove savings.

## Launch and continuation

Use `prompt-manager skill read harness-goal-authoring` for the selected harness's goal composition, transport and proof rules. Keep the goal concise and point at the working brief instead of repeating its design. Use a real native continuation route when the commission needs it. Guidance alone does not supply wakeups or hard limits.

### Generate a multi-study supervisor goal from ordinary language

The operator supplies the mission, priorities and any real limits in ordinary language; the supervisor authors the goal and durable commission. Do not ask the operator to write a polished prompt or fill a generic goal form. Read `harness-goal-authoring` and this skill, then convert the request into one short goal that points at a reviewable cohort packet. The general skill owns goal slots, proof and path syntax; this section supplies the research-specific inputs and portfolio rules.

Before writing the goal, give every study one concise card: user-valued outcome, current evidence and best next direction, freedom to change source/build a demonstration, proof of a consequential result, and any real human-only boundary. Do not replace a sustained question with a checklist, test quota or generic report. Encourage implementation or an end-to-end result when it is the strongest answer; do not impose code changes without evidence.

The current agent is the supervisor, cohort writer and owner of synthesis; do not create a supervisor worker. Workers read `adaptive-research` and relevant method skills before substantive work. Their first checkpoint names the skill IDs and actual source paths used, so the supervisor can audit method without requiring a registry receipt. If `prompt-manager skill read` is unavailable, read the canonical `SKILL.md` from the repository when accessible; record the actual source and continue. A missing receipt is `unverified`, not proof that a worker skipped the skill or a reason to reject useful work or stop the portfolio. Review the work for the method's decisions; do not turn skill-read evidence into a new dispatch gate.

Use the [cohort lifecycle](cohort-lifecycle.md) for current cohort and per-study states. The cohort ledger owns coordination; native goal state is a separate observation. A user-confirmed stop can authorize a fresh commission while unavailable native telemetry remains `unknown`; use fresh identities and clocks, hold only an uncertain dispatch, and keep independent rows moving.

Review material returns against the outcome: inspect changed paths when code was expected, otherwise challenge the evidence and decision. A no-code result needs a clear reason it best answers the question. Do not use line quotas or accept activity, reports or green tests as success by themselves.

Work around friction: diagnose a failed route, then choose a materially different repair, source, tool, execution path or useful pivot. Do not repeat the same failed attempt unless a changed condition or new evidence makes it informative. Keep pursuing safe, authorized options while they can advance the outcome; stop trying routes when none remain useful, record the exact unknown, and keep independent rows moving. Hold only the affected action when another attempt risks duplicate work or external effects. Ask the user only for a consequential human-owned choice, credential, approval or external action with no safe alternative. Never route infrastructure repair to the user. Keep all study rows visible; do not let one busy study hide the others.

Compose the goal by replacing every bracketed value below with facts from the current commission and packet, then check it against `harness-goal-authoring` and the selected harness's actual transport limit and proof path. The goal is a portfolio finish line, not a substitute for the per-study briefs. Never copy predecessor counts, stop claims, durations, allowances or state from an example or prior cohort; source each from the current operator authorization and cohort packet. If one is genuinely unspecified, record it as unknown and ask only when progress or authority depends on the missing value.

```text
Work until each study in [cohort packet] reaches its stated outcome or evidence-backed closure. This session owns supervision, cohort record and synthesis; do not spawn a supervisor. Read research-coordination, harness-goal-authoring and packet. Workers read adaptive-research and named methods, recording sources in checkpoints. Advance or capacity-queue independent studies; hold only uncertain dispatches. Work around friction through owner repair, alternate routes or useful pivots; ask the user only for an unresolved human-owned choice. Follow the packet's allowance and stop conditions; keep cohort, worker and native-goal states separate. Run each study's packet-defined proof; record evidence, changed paths and validation when implementation is expected, remaining/unknown work, cleanup and next action. Leave cumulative synthesis and resume route.
```

A direction-bearing assignment states the outcome, brief and checkpoint paths, authorized freedom, proof, and original stopping conditions. For example, adapt this prose to the actual commission:

> Develop the research direction in the linked working brief until its useful outcome or actual stopping condition is reached. Choose methods and follow consequential implications within its authority and remaining allowance. Treat starting checks as suggestions. Challenge the hypothesis and revise the framing when evidence warrants. Preserve the larger question across checkpoints; one experiment or session does not finish the commission. Show decisive evidence and uncertainty. Checkpoint current findings, next decision, allowance and owned effects before interruption or handoff.

Do not use this example as a substitute for a specific brief. Before accepting a handoff, ask whether a fresh investigator can explain why this direction matters, start substantive work without reconstructing the history, disagree with the hypothesis, and pursue the implications without waiting for a new prompt after every check.
