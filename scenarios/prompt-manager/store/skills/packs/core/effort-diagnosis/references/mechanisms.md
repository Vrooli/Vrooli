# Mechanisms that make long efforts under-deliver

This is the single catalog of failure mechanisms for goals, supervisors and sub-agent trees.
`harness-goal-authoring` and `research-coordination` cite these IDs; they do not restate them.
Each row lists the mechanism, the signature that confirms it, and the owner of the fix.
The evidence column names the run where the mechanism was first measured.

Two facts from `docs/agent-system/LONG_EFFORT_DESIGN.md` §1 frame every row. Change size
follows how long one agent owns one coherent target: workers that live minutes never make large
changes. Agents act on what is near and concrete: procedures beat adjectives, and a long goal or
an early skill read loses force first. In unattended efforts nobody plays the operator's
steering role unless the structure supplies it.

## Catalog

| ID | Mechanism | Signature (how to confirm) | Fix owner |
|---|---|---|---|
| M1 | **Objective and stance loss after compaction.** Codex re-injects a goal's objective only at goal-turn starts. After compaction a long objective becomes `[objective omitted; exceeds the evidence limit]`, and only the last 8 user messages survive (`LONG_EFFORT_DESIGN.md` §4a). A turn can last hours. The harder the agent works without ending a turn, the longer it runs without its goal; when no retained user message carries the stance, nothing does. | `timeline`: compactions marked `objective omitted`, or retaining no operator message; long gaps between `goal` events; behaviour changes right after a re-injection. `drift` tripwires `objective-lost`, `unit-per-turn`, `stance-not-retained`. | Goal text: stance message, kernel ≤600 characters, one unit per turn (`harness-goal-authoring` §2a). |
| M2 | **Precedent ratchet.** Retained context holds the agent's last messages, and the wake or checkpoint shows the latest records. Each smaller unit becomes the template for the next. | Unit size or duration falls monotonically; narration repeats the same closing formula; recent records look alike. | Curated exemplars instead of latest records; metrics against a target in the brief. |
| M3 | **Exit ramps.** The cheapest acceptable terminal condition wins: "evidence-backed closure", "blocked", no-change units that count, `unknown`. | Early `complete` or closure (the research supervisor closed after 1h15 of 20h); many no-change units; 36% of all native goals ended `blocked`; runs closed as "investigate" or "blocked" on a capacity warning (four desktop cohorts, the artist-world study). | Goal text: the agent ends units, never the goal; a fresh challenger decides closure. |
| M4 | **Count destination (Goodhart).** "N rounds" rewards finishing rounds, and a round with no change is the cheapest to finish. | Units per hour rises while changed lines per unit falls. | Outcome destination; no-change units do not count. |
| M5 | **Asymmetric scrutiny.** Reviewers attack diffs; nobody attacks a missing diff. Validation infrastructure that is slow or degraded raises the cost of change further. | Reviewers endorse "no safe change"; narration cites gates (Test Genie admission, coverage, analyzer outages). | A reviewer who argues for change on every no-change unit; repair the gate at its owner. |
| M6 | **Adverb ambition.** Ambition stated as adjectives ("be bold") while caution is stated as procedures (identity, unknown, blocked, checkpoint). The agent executes the procedures and treats the adjectives as tone. | Count goal clauses: procedures for honesty and caution outnumber concrete magnitude. Instruction stack ratios measured 2026-11-06: research-coordination 93 restrictive to 4 expansive words, harness-goal-authoring 153:16, AGENTS.md 58:8. | Destination with magnitude (`harness-goal-authoring` §3 destination and stance rows). |
| M7 | **Scope chosen by the cost-bearer.** The agent that pays for validation, review and risk chooses the size of its own change, so it chooses small. | Plans and briefs say "bounded", "small probe", "first slice" before any evidence of difficulty; commissions an agent wrote cap the build in advance ("at most three candidates", "no production edits", "no downloads"). | Separate scope-setting (architect step, ambition review) from execution. |
| M8 | **Short-worker cap.** The structure ends workers after minutes, so no worker can attempt a large change. "Cheapest agent, fresh worker per outcome" with small outcomes produces this. | `tree`/`summary`: median worker lifetime in minutes; the lifetime table shows no worker ≥1,000 lines. | Effort structure: long-lived owners re-tasked with `followup_task` (`harness-goal-authoring` §5 shapes F and G). |
| M9 | **Inheritance contagion.** `fork_turns=all` children start with the parent's history: its drift, its caution and most of the context window. They compact early and cannot change model. | `first_input_tokens` > 100k for children (39% of first-level children on 2026-10-14..11-05; window 258,400). | Fresh brief generated from the charter, `fork_turns=none`. |
| M10 | **Delegation collapse.** Codex's default `<multi_agent_mode>` hint says "Do not spawn sub-agents unless the user or applicable AGENTS.md/skill instructions explicitly ask" (replaced in Vrooli's config since 2026-11-06; check the rollout for which text an effort saw). When the goal that asked is dropped (M1), the agent stops delegating. Claude-only vocabulary (`implementer`, `opus`) in a Codex goal names nothing. | Spawns stop mid-run; depth-2 workers are rare; no hunters after a compaction. | Every brief says spawning is expected; use the harness's real roles; keep the replacement hint installed (`LONG_EFFORT_DESIGN.md` §4). |
| M11 | **Bookkeeping crowding.** The supervisor's attention goes to identities, allowances, native/cohort/worker state and cleanup instead of driving outcomes. | Supervisor narration dominated by reconciliation; idle slots; polling (research supervisor: hourly feed polling from 10:00, zero workers in hour 10). | Move reconciliation into tools; supervisor is architect and driver (`research-coordination`). |
| M12 | **Evidence grain sets work grain.** A queue of single-command findings produces one-command rounds and never drains, which starves lower-priority focus areas. | Many units map one-to-one onto queue rows; the same queue items reappear; strict focus order never advances. | Cluster findings into classes before assigning; time-share between focus areas; write each verdict back to its finding. |
| M13 | **Frame drift from the latest skill.** The most recently read skill frames the next decision; caution-heavy skills read late override the stance read early. | Skill reads after the method skill's last read; narration adopts another skill's stop rule. | Re-read the method skill and charter at each unit start; name which skill wins on conflict. |
| M14 | **Shared-tree avoidance.** Files other agents touched are treated as off limits, which removes the highest-value territory. | Narration: "dirty", "leave those owner files alone", "shared write area". | Stance line licensing shared files (`AGENTS.md` §2). |
| M15 | **Harness contradictions.** Codex's system prompt says "you will still see all prior user requests" after compaction. Only the last 8 user messages survive, a long one truncated, and a long goal objective does not, so the agent never re-reads the goal. | Agent proceeds after compaction without re-reading the charter. | AGENTS.md rule: re-read the active goal's charter after compaction. |
| M16 | **Real diminishing returns.** The territory really is exhausted. | Large items stay open elsewhere? If yes, this is not M16. Confirm with an independent scan of open high-tier items. | Change territory or destination; close honestly. |
| M17 | **Epistemic framing.** The effort's unit of output is a finding, not a capability: topics, runs, closure rules and vocabulary ("study", "question", "disposition", "evidence-backed closure") were built for knowledge production, so agents produce receipts. | Run folders multiply while landed changes stay few (240 research runs across 21 topics by 2026-11-07, debt-reduction 48 runs with no landed simplification in its summary); topic pages are lists of "does not establish" results; the mission says "decision-ready" where building is authorized. | Programme framing: a `MISSION.md` per topic with end state, scorecard and workstreams (`research-coordination` [missions](../../research-coordination/references/mission.md)). |
| M18 | **Mission erosion.** Each handoff paraphrases the previous checkpoint, so the operator's intent shrinks one rewrite at a time; the prepared commission becomes the agent's summary of its own last small result. | Compare the operator's original words with the current brief: scope words disappear and qualifiers appear ("narrower recommendation is useful", "if justified"). Image Tools: "massively improve the infra" had become "a decision-ready foundation". The same lineage loss re-opens work that already landed and re-queues decisions the operator already answered (first-draft program-runtime and agent-connect missions, 2026-11-07). | Operator intent kept verbatim and append-only in `MISSION.md`; commissions are generated from the mission, not from the last checkpoint. |
| M19 | **Tractability bias.** A high evidence bar is cheapest to meet in a small, testable corner, so the agent picks the corner. | Consecutive runs on one narrow sub-problem while larger workstreams stay untouched (eight Image Tools runs on JPEG metadata branches of one operation). | Workstreams ranked by scorecard movement; the architect pass and ambition review (`LONG_EFFORT_DESIGN.md` §2b) choose scope, not the executor. |
| M20 | **Rigor theater.** Scientific-trial procedure applied to engineering choices: frozen comparators, predeclared thresholds and multi-decimal metrics for decisions that are cheap to reverse. Each step costs more, so fewer and smaller steps happen, and the trial often ends in "no change". | Reports with predeclared thresholds that end in "no default change supported"; trial protocol and disclaimers outweigh the change. | Rigor sized to reversibility ([missions](../../research-coordination/references/mission.md#sizing-rigor-to-the-decision)); one shared evaluator instead of per-run protocols. |
| M21 | **Correction residue.** Each operator correction of an overclaim became a standing rule, and nothing balanced them, so the instruction stack teaches that overclaiming is punished and under-delivery is not. | Hedge phrases outnumber build verbs in topic pages (TV topic: 42 hedges in one 143 KB README); skills dominated by unknown/claim/state rules (M6 ratios). | State both failure directions: under-delivery is reported like overclaiming; honesty rules govern claims, never scope. |
| M22 | **No denominator.** Without a target state, any fixed edge case reads as progress, so small units are never visibly small. | Checkpoints list what changed but never what remains of the end state; no scorecard or workstream table exists. | Scorecard plus workstream table in the charter; checkpoints report dimensions moved (`mission_check.py` renders the portfolio). |
| M23 | **Missing approval loop.** A topic that may only build after operator approval has no working route for proposals, so exploration loops forever and never converts into building. | Zero proposals in the attention view while exploration runs repeat (freelance: 26 runs and repeated hourly screens, no proposal); the attention page is a log where a proposal would be buried. | Proposal packets at the top of `ATTENTION.md`, delivered as decision asks; approval converts a packet into a `build` workstream ([operator attention](../../research-coordination/references/operator-attention.md#proposal-packets)). |
| M24 | **No heartbeat.** The effort's wake source is gone while its workers run on: the agent paused its native goal at the operator's request and only the operator can re-activate it, or the session never had a goal. The scheduler then runs only when the operator types. | `drift` tripwire `paused-goal`; `timeline` shows only `turn_start user` for hours; the agent reports "the goal still reads paused" as a caveat, not as an action; owners receive no message for hours or sit idle after a return (four-programme cohort: 11 h, one owner idle 129 min). | Goal text and method skill: after a pause, the first line of the reply names `/goal resume`, and the scheduler holds its wait inside the turn (`LONG_EFFORT_DESIGN.md` D4). Platform: an Agent Manager root run with a review timer. |
| M25 | **Hot loop.** The scheduler ends its turn while owners are only working, the goal re-injects at once, and each turn repeats the unit-start reads. Waits are short by habit, so every wake is a status check. | `drift` tripwires `hot-loop` and `short-waits`; goal turns of a few minutes; a compaction every 20–30 minutes in a thread that does no domain work; the scheduler's share of tree tokens above 10%; a ledger entry every few minutes titled "delta". | Structure: `min_wait_timeout_ms` in the Codex settings (`LONG_EFFORT_DESIGN.md` §4); `wait_agent` at 3600000 ms; a wake with no return is followed by another wait (D4); owners message the scheduler for four things only (§3). |
| M26 | **Review flood.** The closure procedure is applied to increments: every small step gets an ambition review, a challenger and a re-challenge of its repair. Review becomes the work, and a rejection produces a longer plan document, not a larger change. | `drift` tripwire `review-flood`; child names ending in `_challenger`, `_recheck`, `_counterreview`, `v1.3.x`; zero-line children take most child time; plan files grow while the measured change stays flat. | One ambition review per workstream plan, one challenger per showcase claim; increments are verified by owner tests and the running showcase (D16). |
| M27 | **Check-in capture.** The operator's status questions are the newest retained user messages, so they evict the stance message and become the standing instruction: each goal turn answers the last question again. | A compaction's `retained_context` holds only "how is it going" messages; goal turns open with "You're right, my last report…"; `drift` tripwire `stance-not-retained` with `--stance-marker`. | A status question is answered from the state page and is not steering (D3); the charter re-read carries the stance; the operator can read `STATE.md` without messaging the scheduler. |
| M28 | **Charter bloat.** The file that is re-read at every unit start and after every compaction also holds the ledger, so every record makes every re-read larger. | Charter file size against its first version (18 KB to 177 KB in a day); several reads of the same file per turn; owners instructed to re-read the cohort record. | Charter, state page and ledger are three files (D15); owners read their card and mission only. |
| M29 | **Dead-end showcase.** A showcase needs something agents cannot produce (an operator's review receipt, a week of elapsed time, a GPU the owner itself filled), and the closure rule forbids leaving it, so the owner polishes one case or parks the lane without asking. | The same case through many revisions; a challenger that keeps returning "operator review missing"; GPU work parked for hours with no retry and no request; other workstreams in the same mission untouched (two Image Tools workstreams had no work in 10 h). | The workstream files its one request and is marked `waiting-operator` or `waiting-time`; its owner takes the next workstream (D17). An owner stops what it starts and resolves capacity through the holder ([missions](../../research-coordination/references/mission.md#capacity-and-gates)). |
| M30 | **Silent operator queue.** Requests exist but do not reach the operator: a consent announced once, an item filed under "no response needed", a decision mentioned as a clause in a status paragraph, a proposal written as evidence identifiers. | The operator says nothing arrived; the consent request reads `pending` after hours; the attention page's actionable items are older than the commission; proposals open with hashes. | `Needs you` first in every reply and on the state page, in plain words, with the link that answers each item (D17); the pending consent is its first item. |
| M31 | **Self-set exam.** The agent that builds a deliverable also writes the target it is tested on, sized so that the deliverable passes. The work is then measured, compared and reviewed against that target, so every check is green and nothing shows the deliverable is thin. No one asks what a real target looks like or what reusable test environment the kind of work needs. | Test inputs counted in single digits and authored in the same run as the deliverable (Freelance F2: a 128-line scraper against a three-record local page; eight CSV rows; a 95-line sample app); deliverables authored in minutes (six in one worker's 25 minutes) while measuring and review take most of the time (building at most 26% of 553 child minutes; 29 of 52 children were reviewers); no real instance, established framework or platform test capability in the commands; a summary that says no capability is missing; the operator defers the work on sight. | Destination: the showcase names a proving ground that a resource or scenario owns and real targets at real size; work is organised by category, not by sample ([proving grounds](../../research-coordination/references/mission.md#proving-grounds)). A review page shows the code. |

Check M16 last and only after the others are ruled out: in every measured case large items
were still open.
