Switchboard is used in two postures that must not be blended. Conversing happens on a phone, in an app judged against iMessage and Telegram — where any invention reads as a bug. Governing happens at a desk, in an instrument panel where invention is welcome if it makes a risk legible. Every decision below follows from keeping those apart.

What the design is accountable for

Three things, in this order

First

You can tell what the agent may do, before it does it

Refusals, gates and grants are written for someone judging a risk — never as a permission error. This is the only part of the product that can cause harm, so it gets the most design attention and the most direct language.

Second

You can tell who wrote a message

Authorship is functional, not decorative. The loop breaker in OT-P1-004 depends on it, and a person must be able to see what the loop breaker sees.

Third

You can tell which channel you are in, without reading it

A cross-channel list where the channel lives only in the detail pane forces a click to answer the first question anybody has.

The marker

▾ before an agent's name

A small caret, not a colour and not a badge. It survives greyscale, it survives a screenshot, and it costs no horizontal space in a group where three authors are already competing for the gutter.

Silence is legible

An agent that says nothing must say why

OT-P1-003 makes silence correct behaviour in a group. Correct behaviour that looks like a dead bot is still a product failure, so suppressed turns leave a quiet, dismissible trace.

The refusal

Withheld, why, and what unblocks it

Three facts, in that order, addressed to a person by name, with the next move offered as a button. OT-P1-002 forbids answering partially — so the refusal itself has to carry the information.

The gate is answered in place. No modal, no navigation, no separate approvals queue to remember. It carries its own expiry because an unanswered prompt that waits forever is indistinguishable from a broken thread — and answering it returns focus to the composer.
The budget appears only under pressure. A meter that is always on screen becomes furniture and stops being read. It surfaces when it is close to silencing the agent, which is the only moment it is information rather than decoration.
Visual language

Three encodings, each structurally true

The palette is functional. Nothing is coloured to look designed; every encoding carries a fact the system actually depends on.

Channel

An edge, never a fill

iMessage
Telegram
Slack
In-app

A 2px leading edge and a focus ring. A list of filled, differently-coloured chips is unreadable at roster density and makes every new channel a colour-conflict problem. The accent comes from the descriptor, so adding a channel adds a colour without touching the console.

Agent

The descriptor's own triple

appearance.body, head and accent already exist on every prompt-manager agent descriptor. Rendering from those exact values makes the roster scannable by shape. Hashing a name instead would make the console and the descriptor disagree about who an agent is.

Trust

A rank, not a category

stranger
known
trusted
owner

The room ceiling is a minimum across tiers, so tiers have to look comparable or the rule cannot be understood. An ascending ladder does that; four arbitrary colours do not. stranger is styled as the ordinary default, never as an alarm — every unrecognised sender is one by design.

Mockup · the dangerous screen

Grants get more space than they need

Spacing is how a design says slow down without a warning dialog. The grant section on an agent is deliberately looser than its information density warrants, and it is read before channels, budgets or activity.

The rule behind the lock

A disabled toggle is a lie

OT-P0-011 makes owner-only capabilities unreachable by construction. A greyed-out switch tells the operator the opposite — that the grant exists on a spectrum they could move. So it is not rendered as a control at all.

Language

Plain first, scope second

"Draft plans, without finalising them" is judgeable. plan:draft is not, to anyone who has not read the scope catalogue. Both are shown; only one is the label.

Ownership

The profile is a pointer

Editing links out to prompt-manager rather than offering a local editor, because a local editor forks the descriptor the moment somebody uses it.

Copy is design material

This product refuses things constantly

So refusal copy is a first-class surface, not error-handling debris. Every refusal answers three questions in order: what was withheld, why, and what would unblock it.

Never Error: insufficient permissions (403) Something went wrong. Please try again. This action is not available.

Names nothing, blames nobody, offers no move. Indistinguishable from a bug, which is how a governed system loses the trust its governance was for.

Always Not in this room — Sam is known, and account detail needs trusted. Matt, I can send it to you directly. Stayed silent — nobody addressed me. Say @wren and I'll answer. Out of turns for this thread until 14:00. Raise the budget in Settings.

Specific, addressed to a person, and ends on the next move. The same register whether it is a tier, a ceiling or a budget.

The state contract

Four rules that are expensive to get wrong

RuleWhat it prevents
Empty is not an errorAn empty region is perceivably distinct from a failed one and from a loading one. Where empty means nothing needs attention, it says so in a sentence — zeroed charts read as a broken page, a sentence reads as good news.
Partial keeps workingOne failed region names what failed and leaves every other region interactive. A page does not become unusable because a sidebar could not load.
Stale is labelled, never hiddenA cached figure carries its age. No number appears without its freshness when the read is stale.
A disabled control states whyA composer disabled because the adapter is offline says that, and says what would re-enable it. A disabled control with no reason is indistinguishable from a bug.
These are enforced, not aspirational as a group. Every page spec declares its states, and experience-manager spec validate checks that declaration against the required UX-state contract in DESIGN.md. Individual claims are tiered aspirational and manual today because the UI does not exist yet — machine tier needs stable selectors. Promoting them as surfaces get built is how this contract tightens.
The component library

What RCL gives us, and what it does not

Measured across 167 catalogued components. Most of this UX is buildable from the library today — with one whole family missing and one live bug.

Adopt as-is

Five that are genuinely ready

ApprovalPrompt (527 lines, 7 stories including permission-denied and retry) already models the capability gate almost exactly — it is a wrapper, not a new component. Avatar (9 stories), VoiceInputButton (7 states), AsyncPanel (5 stories matching the region lifecycle exactly), EmptyState (3).

The false friend

Message is not a chat message

Its catalog id is ai.message and its contract is an AI-console transcript card: a bordered, elevated panel with 32px padding and a 72ch measure, taking certainty, urgency, citations and activity. Right for an agent console. No sidedness, no author grouping, no day divider, no delivery state. And one anatomy-only story with zero declared arg fields.

A live bug worth fixing this week. experience-manager spec validate raises a hard error for pinning experience-surface@1.0.0 "without a canonical experience contract". The contract exists — at 1.0.3. The react-vite template pins 1.0.0. That is 24 pins across 5 scenarios (browser-automation-studio, hello-mobile, infrastructure-manager, landing-page-business-suite, switchboard), so every generated scenario ships an experience spec that fails validation for following the pattern its own README tells it to follow. One-line template fix plus a re-pin.
The systemic one: half the catalogue is anatomy only. 86 of 167 components (51%) carry one story or fewer, and only 41 of 167 (25%) have their own experience contract. A one-story component can only ever have one specified state, because component specs anchor each state to a named story. AttachmentPreview, BudgetBar, MasterDetail, DataTable, CollectionPage and Timeline are all in that group, and all six are on this UX's path.
