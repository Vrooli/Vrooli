The corrected version — now with an unmistakable capture affordance (the + FAB), separate from the "act on next step" bar. Two different intents, two different controls.

New — frictionless capture: the FAB (mobile) and ⌘K command palette (desktop, from any page) open a natural-language box — type "lunch with Sam tue 1pm 1h" and it parses into a placed item. This is the single feature that most separates a delightful planner from a tedious one.

Concept: you're standing at the observatory, reading tonight's chart. Desktop shows the whole sky; mobile shows the path in front of you.

Focus

Focus is the page that's better on mobile than desktop — a single, all-consuming task. The desktop wastes space with one small timer; mobile makes the timer the entire world, which is exactly right for a focus session.

New: the timer now has a Countdown mode (pre-filled from the task's planned duration) alongside Stopwatch. When the countdown hits 0 it rolls into overtime counting up in amber — so you both commit to a target and honestly capture the overrun (which feeds the learning loop, below).

Concept: holding a heading. One star, dead ahead, everything else dimmed.

Plan

The worst desktop→mobile offender in the current build: 8 view modes crammed into tabs. On desktop that richness is a strength (a full command chart); on mobile it's paralysis. Mobile collapses to Day/Week + a capacity read + one timeline, with placement, forecast, commitments and routines demoted to sheets.

Concept: the week as a star chart — capacity is the horizon, commitments are fixed stars, placement is plotting a new course.

Goals

Goals is a list surface, so mobile divergence is light — a vertical list is already the native shape. The lift here is conceptual: make goals feel like the fixed stars you steer by, not generic progress cards. Progress meters become the constellation you're completing.

Concept: each goal is a star; milestones are the points of light that form it. Completing milestones lights up the constellation.

Review

Review mixes a narrative showpiece (the compass) with a data table (the week breakdown). Tables are the classic desktop→mobile trap: on mobile a table becomes a card list, never a horizontally-scrolling grid.

New — the learning loop lives here: the Calibration card is the aggregate home for "learning from planning." It reads your estimate-vs-actual history and tells you your bias (e.g. you underestimate deep work ~30%) and whether your estimating is improving over time. Per-goal variance badges (see Goals) are the detail; this is the summary. Same data an agent can read to plan better for you.

Concept: taking a reading. Where did you actually go this week vs. the course you set?

Settings ← the outlier — biggest redesign upside

The audit flagged Settings as "functional but generic — infrastructure, not experience." The fix isn't just prettier forms; it's giving Settings the same world as the rest of the app. Reframed as "Calibrate your instrument": theme is the sky you observe under, availability is when the observatory is open, integrations are your feeds.

Concept: tuning the observatory. Even the config screen belongs to the world.
