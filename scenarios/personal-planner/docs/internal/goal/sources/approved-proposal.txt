Recommendation and current answer
Keep the Observatory direction and concentrate the next implementation decision on a trustworthy daily loop. The current product is more than a visual shell: synthetic work capture persisted, an Open timer session survived pause and reopening, and its ended state, active time and note were independently read from the isolated database. But the ordinary path is not ready to rely on. Default Countdown cannot start, and accepting a feasible placement hangs without saving. A well-presented forecast or capacity card cannot compensate for those failures.
The strongest next design makes the next useful action obvious, distinguishes accepted facts from drafts, and gives saving and recovery the same care as the scenic interface. Retain the expressive typography, calm palette and observatory setting. Reduce large instructional banners, restore useful content above the phone fold, keep completion attached to the task/session it affects, and show precise status rather than generic reassurance.
This dossier separates observed runtime, current source diagnosis, accepted product requirements and proposed design. Proposed screens are synthetic and unimplemented. The owner released the investigation and private design-document work; that is not approval of these pixels or behavior, permission to implement the proposals, or certification of R1. The scope is one coherent review, with testing, design and reusable capability learning inside it.
Two bounded runtime sweeps now establish eight concrete defects and a complete P01–P42 disposition inventory. The second sweep operated fifteen BAS flows, including failed attempts retained as evidence. This candidate incorporates those results; independent substantive and visual critique informed the revisions; exact delivery checks are recorded with the final artifact. Product readiness remains blocked, while the reusable access/testing capability is established within its documented scope.

Observatory continuity and the main design gap
The accepted direction has a clear identity: an expressive serif date, narrow navigation, one prominent next action, quieter capacity/commitment information, a truthful time horizon and a scenic place that remains in the background. The observed runtime retains meaningful parts of that direction. It should not be dismissed as a generic dashboard merely because it needs substantial interaction repair.
The working screens nevertheless spend considerable space describing their own intentions. “A day you can keep,” “A truthful look back,” large Signals/learning banners and repeated explanation panels compete with the actual task. The most valuable content is the action, the relevant numbers, what changed and what can be done next. Retain the necessary explanation through short local labels and progressive disclosure; avoid turning every screen into an instruction sheet.
The detached Mark complete button below the next-action surface weakens the relationship between action and object. The runtime’s capacity uses available and breathing room without a nearby decomposition; this invites a mistaken reading that all blank or unallocated time is spare. The proposed Today design keeps the principal action inside its task card, places secondary actions in context and explicitly labels whole-day capacity. Detailed calculation belongs one step away, not in an ambiguous third total.

End should close the session and present a small outcome decision at the boundary. Done, More to do and Blocked answer different questions. Distinguish session lifecycle from segment type and task status. Running, paused and ended describe the session; work, break, interruption and unclassified describe segments; open, blocked and done describe work. A break must not silently start counting active work while the person is away. A concurrent second device should reconcile to one authoritative current session. If intervals overlap, ask whether one corrects or duplicates the other. Recompute effective totals and learning eligibility; never automatically assign the elapsed overnight period to productive work.

Commitments capacity and wider product rules
A commitment is an explicit promise with a result, completion criteria and boundary. A forecast is a derived outlook under current assumptions. A changed forecast must not rewrite an original/current promise or imply that another person agreed to a revision. Show original promise, latest accepted revision, forecast, freshness and acknowledgment status as separate fields.
Capacity is both an interval set and a budget. Fixed obligations subtract their union, not the sum of overlaps. Reserve is counted once. Date-assigned work consumes demand even without a clock time; converting part to a timed session must remove the same dated demand. Forecast placements are hypothetical and do not become accepted reservations. Unknown estimates remain visible in the queried scope instead of becoming zero.
Source apps retain domain ownership. Personal Planner owns accepted schedule allocations and private planning records; a source app owns its content and authoritative completion. A meal’s unattended cooking or an agent’s runtime is not automatically exclusive human work. Filtered views must still account for other authorized busy time. A source outage retains last-known constraints and freshness, not an empty calendar.
The wider R1 design also needs routine occurrence identity, civil-time/DST rules, read-only provider health, explicit sharing previews, narrow recipient-bound guest views, export/import previews and safe restored state. They matter to a complete personal product, but are not reasons to call the first daily-loop repair a full R1 release. The coverage inventory keeps those obligations discoverable without inventing current implementation.

A repeatable bounded sequence
1. Identify the machine, project owner, checkout/dirty tree, served build and relevant source instructions. Record current process health and active consumers before a lifecycle change.
2. Choose read-only live or mutating shadow by the intended effect. Verify the actual target storage and upstream. Inspect aggregate live and shadow baselines without copying private live records.
3. Run a tiny uniquely identified synthetic canary only in shadow. Retain its ID and read it back independently. Track every later synthetic object, keeping effects bounded to the stated cases.
4. Prefer BAS for repeatable journeys and local browser interaction for exploration. Keep both run receipts. A page body includes hidden text and CSS; use visible scoped state assertions and independent API/database confirmation for material writes.
5. Inspect every step outcome. A BAS terminal completed label can coexist with failed or misleading assertions. Preserve failed attempts and the corrected flow rather than replacing their history.
