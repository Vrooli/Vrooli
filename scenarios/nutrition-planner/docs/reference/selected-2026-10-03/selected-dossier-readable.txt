The whole experience
Product purpose and the limits of this proposal
Nooch helps a person decide what to eat, arrange a realistic week, shop, use their kitchen and cook with less recurring effort. Nutrition, cost, variety and dietary rules inform those decisions, but none should require a perfect food diary or pantry census before the product becomes useful. The proposed experience is meal-first and progressively detailed. It should remain honest and capable when quantities, prices, targets, equipment details or external providers are missing.
SOURCE REQUIREMENT
R02 retains the full food loop. Nooch is not solely a dinner gallery, calorie logger or calendar. Today, Week, Meals, Groceries and Kitchen remain the five primary destinations in that order. Provider-dependent generation, lookup and calendar integration have explicit fallbacks. Public publishing, automated purchasing, commercial billing and unrestricted scene editing are outside the core redesign.

The source foundation already contains extensive behavior and engineering defaults. This dossier develops their interaction consequences, corrects cross-surface inconsistencies and presents unresolved choices. Source requirements are not all individually pinned owner approvals. New boards, exact wording, visual composition and recommended defaults remain proposals. The owner-confirmed Team Anteaters reference establishes desired involvement and depth; its theatre architecture and original document format do not define Nooch.
The primary audience is the owner making design choices and a future implementer needing a coherent basis. A design-inspection pass can establish internal consistency and readable specifications. It cannot prove that the current nutrition-planner application behaves this way. Current signed-in implementation, migration safety and production accessibility remain separately verifiable work.
The first useful outcome
Recommendation. Begin with a person who can save valid settings, choose or capture a meal, make an understandable plan change and confirm what was actually purchased. Missing recipes, stock quantities, prices or provider access should produce a usable fallback rather than fabricated values. Follow First useful session → Planning a realistic week → Shopping and purchase reconciliation, then One meal across the whole product for the connected consistency check.
Decision dependency. NQ-001, NQ-002 and NQ-003 govern this first review slice; NQ-006 governs authoritative effects. Immersive coverage, calendar changes and full export defaults remain important later review slices. This is a proposed reading path through the whole-product design, not an approved release cut or implementation schedule.
A source requirement appears in a green treatment with its locator. A recommendation appears in blue. An owner question uses amber and names alternatives, recommendation and tradeoffs. A defect or evidence gap uses red with consequence and proposed repair. Color is a scanning aid; the labels and IDs carry the meaning. Settled design is reserved for an explicit decision with its scope and source.
Navigation that preserves the task
The five destinations answer different questions. Today answers what to do next. Week answers whether the plan fits. Meals answers what can be made or discovered. Groceries answers what remains to acquire. Kitchen answers what is available and what constraints matter. Global Settings contains appearance, artwork, provider usage, integration, units, account and data transfer; it should not absorb food preferences merely because they are settings-like.
A navigation change must preserve meaningful local context. Opening a recipe from Wednesday dinner retains that date, slot and intended action. Returning to Explore restores its filters and Add versus Replace context. Switching Week from Meals to Nutrition preserves the same occurrences and dates. A browser back action should not create another meal, abandon a draft silently or reset a shopping trip.
RECOMMENDED DESIGN
Maintain explicit contextual banners for target-specific browsing. A banner such as “Replacing Wednesday dinner” includes a clear exit. Leaving the context changes the available action to ordinary discovery; it never silently replaces the old target after the user believes they have exited. The destination itself remains the same Meals area, avoiding parallel unexplained copies of Explore.

ISSUE OR GAP
Historical compact references show four tabs and omit Kitchen. Correcting this is not a new style preference: R01 explicitly requires five labeled destinations. The new compact shell must also avoid a five-tab bar competing with a second persistent action bar. Focused cooking intentionally changes navigation, but retains Exit cooking and View full recipe.

Connected lifecycle example
A person saves a Sesame tofu bowl as a draft, later adds sufficient quantities and a viable stovetop method, places its selected revision on Monday, and reserves a leftover portion for Tuesday. The grocery list derives the missing ingredients without consuming pantry stock. Confirmed purchases add actual acquired quantities. Cooking records a batch, and optional intake consumes a prepared portion. Editing the recipe on Wednesday creates a new revision; Monday's batch and intake keep their original basis. Each chapter below must preserve this same identity chain.
First useful session
Save useful progress without false readiness
The four source-defined steps are Your food, Your kitchen, Your rhythm and Ready. They should be a short introduction to decisions that influence the next meal, not an exhaustive survey. Food rules distinguish hard exclusions from dislikes. Kitchen records selected capabilities without guessing appliances. Rhythm introduces effort, variety, cost preferences and flexible slots. Targets, detailed inventory, supplements and retailer preferences can follow when relevant.
SOURCE REQUIREMENT
Setup drafts are separate from active settings until applied. Explore first is allowed with an explicit unconfigured state. No personalized nutrition targets, allergies, stock or supplement doses are copied from illustrative fixtures. A household serving count does not create identical personal nutrition profiles.

RECOMMENDED DESIGN
Ready presents the saved configuration and actual candidate availability as separate outcomes. A successful settings save may coexist with a blocked automatic plan. If there are no eligible meals, say so and provide capture and clearly labeled sample routes. Do not disable saving valid preferences because the recipe corpus is empty. Do not encourage weakening a hard rule merely to populate a week.

Requirements for each step
Your food needs selectable rules with explanations and a way to add or edit a rule. A hard exclusion and a disliked ingredient must look and behave differently. If a rule cannot be evaluated against current recipe data, the outcome is Needs information. A polished green “Fits” label is not justified by absence of a detected conflict.
Your kitchen exposes tiles as the complete authoritative interface. The scene is supportive context. Zero appliances is valid and should allow assembly or ready-to-eat suggestions where their evidence is sufficient. A stove and oven are distinct capabilities; a combined range can supply both. Do not silently select equipment in a real new account because the demo kitchen shows it.
Your rhythm can start with a small number of preference prompts, but it must preserve an existing custom vector during editing. A preset changes only the intended soft preferences. Required exclusions and hard targets do not become weaker in Easy mode. A temporary low-effort preference carries an explicit scope, such as this meal or through a date, rather than becoming a hidden permanent profile change.
Ready summarizes what is known and what remains optional. Apply has a precise save boundary. A local offline draft says it is stored on this device or queued, not server-confirmed. A failure retains the draft and exposes Retry. Successful application returns a stable receipt/state and a useful next action; reopening should not restart the questionnaire.
Unconfigured and incomplete states
An unconfigured Explore screen can show clearly identified examples. It cannot say they fit allergies that have not been entered. A partially configured profile can evaluate only the supported rules, with the unevaluated portion visible. A valid profile with an empty collection has no candidate generation outcome, while retaining all saved work.
ISSUE OR GAP
NF-001: the earlier packet's no-candidate block did not clearly separate automatic generation from saving settings. The proposed screen resolves that ambiguity, but future implementation must test the save, leave, reopen and recovery paths. A static success screen is not persistence evidence.

The first-session acceptance walkthrough includes empty collection, excluded candidates, unresolved ingredient amounts, save failure, offline queued save and a later added valid recipe. It should show exactly which operation is blocked and which remains available. The design passes this walkthrough only if the user can move forward without false assurances or repeated setup.

Today and a tired evening
The next useful action
Today leads with a selected occurrence: local date and slot, meal identity, active preparation time, total elapsed time and the appropriate primary action. It does not assume that dinner is always next. Configured slots and user selection determine the focus. Where timing is absent, show the slot and let the person choose instead of claiming to know their present activity.
The hero supports immersive scene, editorial photo and minimal no-photo treatments within one action structure. A missing or incompatible image must not move the controls, leave an empty dominant rectangle or make the meal look unfinished. The source requires a deterministic fallback; theme switching changes presentation, not recipe, portion or date.
SOURCE REQUIREMENT
Start cooking pins recipe revision, method and yield. A ready-to-eat product may use View meal or Log meal. A draft without instructions opens an incomplete detail with Edit, not an empty cooking session. The presence of a food photograph never determines readiness.

The seven-day strip is a shortcut to planned meals, not a second calendar authority. Selecting Tuesday updates the hero's explicit date and slot. A compact full-day section beneath it retains breakfast, lunch, dinner, snacks, recurring foods and supplements. This prevents a dinner-first composition from erasing the full-day product requirement.
Swap without collateral changes
A tired-evening flow begins with immediate suitable alternatives. Optional reasons help ranking, but Missing ingredients is temporary context rather than a permanent dislike. A low-effort choice must still satisfy required restrictions and a viable method. Candidates should communicate the comparable basis: active effort, elapsed time, stock uncertainty and material plan consequences.
RECOMMENDED DESIGN
Show a short comparison focused on what changes now. “15 minutes active, 25 minutes elapsed” is more useful than an unlabeled single time. If the swap removes tomorrow's reserved leftover lunch, say that beside the option. A compatible isolated swap may apply quickly with conditional Undo; a material conflict opens ImpactPreview.

Occurrence actions and scope
View recipe, Move, Lock, Change servings, Skip, Remove and Record intake have different consequences. Removing a future occurrence does not delete the recipe or erase past intake. Copying an occurrence creates a new identity and never duplicates actual consumption. Moving onto an occupied slot asks whether to add alongside, swap or replace; drag-and-drop is a shortcut, not the only accessible route.
A planned item already partially consumed cannot be treated like an untouched future card. Show its recorded portion and explain the remaining planned quantity. Editing future intent preserves actual history. Undo must be conditional on whether dependent state has changed; a stale Undo should offer a reviewed correction rather than silently reverting someone else's work.
Today states worth designing
Empty Today offers Choose a meal or Plan my week, without displaying the synthetic bowl as personal data. An intentionally open/social meal is an explicit choice, not a missing assignment. A no-photo meal remains typographically complete. Unknown active time reads Time not set. A completed meal remains inspectable after recipe edits. A full-day nutrition view identifies Planned, Recorded or Expected and its completeness.
ISSUE OR GAP
Retained images use historical Daily branding, occasional non-vegan demo meals and incomplete compact navigation. Preserve those files as evidence of atmosphere, but do not use them as exact new screen contracts. New boards should use one coherent vegan fixture, Nooch identity and the same five destinations.

The decisive walkthrough is a low-effort swap with a dependent leftover and an unavailable ingredient quantity. It should preserve hard rules, show the dependency, avoid claiming a precise shopping impact from qualitative stock, and return the user to the correct selected meal after confirmation or cancellation.

Planning a realistic week
One plan with three lenses
Meals, Nutrition and Time & cost are views over the same week and occurrence identities. The selected local date range remains stable when switching. A day counts as fully planned only relative to its configured required slots or intentional open states. One dinner is not a complete day unless the summary explicitly says dinner scope.
The wide Meals view uses seven day columns and flexible slot rows. Multiple foods can share one slot without being forced into a composite recipe. A smoothie and toast remain separate items when that is how the person thinks about them. Locked, leftover, prep-ahead and eating-out states have explicit labels. Unknown time never sorts as fastest.
Compact Day is the default readable view; All week shows seven stacked sections. It is not a seven-column miniature. The same Add, Move, Lock and servings actions remain available without drag gestures. Day changes retain meaningful local editing state, while an unsaved draft explains whether it follows the day or remains a separate proposal.
Plan proposal and application
Plan my week creates a draft for unlocked future slots. Accepted routine anchors and recorded history remain. The proposal explains changed, unchanged and locked scope; it does not ask the owner to reconsider every unchanged meal. Nutrition, effort, cost and grocery effects are scoped to the same proposal and expose missing evidence.

Leftovers and shared prep
A producer and its later consumers refer to one batch identity. “From Monday” and “For Tuesday lunch” are reciprocal navigable relationships. Moving a producer later than its consumer is not a cosmetic calendar edit; reject the invalid chronology or offer a concrete repair. Removing a consumer releases its reservation without discarding food.
When actual yield is lower than planned, outstanding reservations need reconciliation. A four-portion plan that produces three portions cannot keep four reserved meals looking fulfilled. The batch review should show the shortfall and offer resizing, replacement or an explicit unresolved future meal. This is a cross-chapter contract linking cooking completion, Week and groceries.
Prep for the week groups tasks only where methods and preparation states support sharing. Two recipes containing rice do not necessarily share one batch. Wash greens may be reusable; baking incompatible items simultaneously may not be. Show contributing meals, known active and elapsed time, and storage assumptions. Schedule prep creates a proposal through a verified adapter, never a silently completed task.
Nutrition and time cost views
Nutrition rows identify nutrient, unit, amount or known subtotal, target bounds, period, evidence completeness and selected scope. Expected combines recorded past intake with explicitly remaining planned quantities. It must not add the entire original plan to actual intake. A weekly average cannot erase a daily upper-bound breach. No universal green completeness score should hide missing products or quantities.
Time & cost separates active work, elapsed duration and cleanup. Shared prep is counted once; reheating is separate. A timeline is justified only where timing and dependencies exist. Otherwise display per-meal/day estimates without implying a solved schedule. Portion cost and incremental checkout are different measures, with currency and price age. An old price is dated evidence, not a live quote. Accepted NQ-014 A (ND-011): the cost criterion ranks the next grocery bill after credible stock and package purchases; consumed-food/portion cost remains separately visible. Unknown prices never become free. Cost is still one preference within suitable plans, not permission to trade away hard constraints. Owner evidence: Sentinel_a71916c9c6248191af1b9c8dc1b4dd8d.
ISSUE OR GAP
Historical Week boards show Meals and a compact Day view, while Nutrition and Time & cost mostly exist as labels. Requirements are present in prose, but these tab bodies need concrete design resolution. New proposals should show incomplete evidence, not fill every cell with persuasive fictional precision.

The critical walkthrough starts with two locked meals, a leftover pair and a missing price. Replan only the unlocked future scope, then change one stock fact before Apply. The document's screens, state diagram and calculation example must agree on which rows require review and which remain unchanged.

Finding and shaping meals
Collection and discovery serve different purposes
Your meals is the person's saved collection. Explore offers candidates from curated and user-approved material, with reasons connected to a target slot or current priorities. A favorite is personal metadata; saving creates or links a stable record; recommendation eligibility is a separate assessment. These must not collapse into one heart icon or one undifferentiated status.
SOURCE REQUIREMENT
A name-only meal can be saved as Draft. A meal that conflicts with current rules may remain in the collection. It must not become an automatically recommended compliant recipe. A manual unresolved plan note is allowed only with its unresolved status intact. The product should help capture imperfect information without pretending it is ready.

Collection cards show identity, trustworthy time and a small number of supported tags. Quick meals uses a visible active-time threshold. High protein needs an explicit policy and sufficient evidence; a food name or generated photo does not justify the badge. Unknown numeric fields form an explicit group when sorting, rather than making incomplete meals appear fastest or cheapest.
The empty collection should not feel like a failure. Offer Add meal, paste rough text and labeled samples. Search with no result retains the query and allows clearing individual filters. Archived and Draft records remain discoverable through deliberate filters; they are not silently mixed into the normal Ready collection.
Quick capture and the full editor
RECOMMENDED DESIGN
Start Add meal with a small capture surface, then open structured groups only as needed. Name and rough text establish a durable draft. Yield and time, ingredients, method, nutrition and source/notes are independent sections with clear incompleteness. A long form that requires every field before Save would contradict the source's low-effort capture goal.

Ingredient quantities may be left blank; offer optional suggestions such as pinch, dash or to taste under ND-010. These suggestions never supply invented grams or complete nutrient values. The original source text remains visible beside proposed extraction. An imported “a handful” stays unresolved until the person supplies a basis; it is not converted into an arbitrary gram value. An empty numeric field differs from zero. A failed parser retains the text and allows manual editing. An asynchronous extraction must not overwrite a newer typed amount or changed ingredient identity.
Ingredient changes invalidate only the relevant assessments. A changed amount affects nutrition and cost; an ingredient identity can affect required rules and method suitability. Editing a personal note should not trigger an unnecessary allergen re-review or a new image job. The editor explains material invalidation near the affected section, without requiring the person to understand dependency graphs.
A saved recipe keeps a stable identity and receives an immutable revision. Future occurrences can stay pinned or adopt the new revision through an impact choice. Past cooking and intake retain the original. Adapt creates a draft fork with provenance, not an in-place mutation of a public source or another person's recipe.
Explore reasons should be inspectable
A contextual banner states Add to Wednesday lunch or Replace Wednesday dinner and offers a clear exit. Candidate reasons are structured facts: known active time, ingredients used, viable equipment, compatible current rules, or a dated price basis. “Uses your broccoli” does not mean “you have enough broccoli.” Missing data cannot be translated into a confident affordability badge.
The source defines sections such as For your week, Use what you have, Quick meals, Something different, Cook once eat twice and Affordable additions. Show a section only when there is meaningful evidence and a useful result. An empty carousel should collapse into a concise explanation or relevant action. Endless inspiration is not a substitute for deciding what to eat.
Save, Add, Replace, Adapt and Favorite remain distinct. Add to week preserves a usable pinned recipe record and creates the occurrence coherently. Save twice should not duplicate it. Replace previews material effects and revalidates a target changed on another device. AI meal idea is a separate optional draft workflow; it is not required for ordinary discovery.

Shopping and purchase reconciliation
Review before the aisle
Review mode explains the scope of the list: date range or selected meals, contributing requirements, exact or qualitative stock, missing amounts, package choices and price coverage. Shop mode emphasizes readable rows, quick pickup state and remaining needs. Both modes exist on wide and compact screens; the old desktop Review and phone Shop boards are examples, not exclusive device capabilities.
The list mixes derived ingredients and deliberately added manual items without losing either identity. A plan change can remove an unpurchased derived requirement, but cannot erase soap added manually or a purchase already recorded. A row expansion explains which meals contributed and which conversion/product assumption produced the displayed amount.
SOURCE REQUIREMENT
Have this records a stock assertion with its evidence basis. A shopping checkmark records picked up. Purchase confirmation records acquisition. Intake records eating. The interface must make these distinctions without forcing the person through a technical ledger.

A quantity-aware purchase review
The proposed confirmation screen shows product/package, amount picked up, optional actual total paid in a stated currency, and resulting effects. Picking up one 400 g pack leaves 300 g still to acquire; actual on-hand remains 100 g until confirmation. Confirming it makes on-hand 500 g once, if inventory tracking is enabled. A separate second whole-pack acquisition produces 900 g on hand. The earlier 700 g requirement / 600 g shortage / 200 g remainder example is retained as a distinct priced calculation example in Domain integrity and recovery; it is not mixed into this trace.

Changes while shopping
A plan edit produces Added, Changed and No longer needed groups. Preserve picked-up, fulfilled and manual quantities. Increased requirements expose only the added amount; they do not reset the whole list or pretend the original checkmark covers a larger demand. The person can inspect the contributing recipe revision before accepting the recalculation.
Two devices can act on the same pending pickup. A request idempotency key handles a repeat of the same operation, but two distinct operation IDs need an additional pending-quantity/version boundary. The design must show a refreshed confirmation when another device already claimed the amount. A genuine second purchase remains possible through a new explicit acquisition, not by replaying the first checkbox.
Correction preserves history and previews affected stock and costs. If some acquired quantity has already been consumed or used in a batch, a correction cannot simply delete the receipt and create impossible negative stock. It needs a reviewed adjustment or explicit unresolved reconciliation. The domain chapter supplies the transactional meaning; the screen must express the consequence in ordinary language.
Price and substitution
Price book records product, package, retailer, amount, currency, date, conditions and source. Unit comparisons require compatible units and products. An old promotion cannot be silently treated as today's universally available price. If no prices exist, invite Add prices and show the known requirement without a fabricated total.
Portion cost and checkout are different lenses. In the separate priced calculation example, buying two 400 g packs at USD 2.40 costs USD 4.80 at checkout; allocating 700 g at that basis is USD 4.20. The remaining amount is stock, not a rounding error. Those prices are not silently assigned to the core visual trace, where actual prices remain unknown. Domain integrity and recovery
Purpose and authority
This chapter specifies the proposed behavior that keeps Nooch's planning, shopping, cooking and nutrition views consistent. It describes a design, not verified implementation. Its source basis is the September 22 redesign specification R12–R15 and R21–R28, together with Appendix A DOM-02–05, NUT-02–04, CST-01–03, INV-01–03 and SYS-02. Requirements explicitly present there are identified below as source requirements. Interaction resolutions and additional boundary rules are recommendations for owner review.
The central distinction is between intentions, observations and effects. Scheduling dinner is an intention. Saying there is some rice in the pantry is an observation. Confirming a purchase or a cooked batch creates an inventory effect. None should silently stand in for another. This separation permits a lightweight experience without making its numbers misleading.
The identities behind the experience
A recipe has a stable identity and immutable revisions. A revision contains its ingredients, yield, preparation alternatives, source assertions and calculation basis. A planned occurrence points to a specific recipe revision, date, slot and portion count. A cooking session pins a method and scale; a prepared batch records actual output; a consumption event records what was eaten. These source-required distinctions preserve history when a recipe changes tomorrow.
This conceptual model describes relationships without prescribing database table names. An implementation may use its existing aggregates and transactional conventions so long as these meanings survive. A recipe revision may offer several methods, but an occurrence or cooking session uses one selected viable method. Equipment eligibility asks whether at least one complete method works with available capabilities. It must not require all appliances mentioned across mutually exclusive methods.
A physical device can provide several capabilities. A combined range may offer an oven and cooktop; removing oven access does not remove its burners. Conversely, selecting two capabilities on one multicooker does not create two independent physical devices available simultaneously. Capacity and parallel-use constraints matter when a weekly plan schedules overlapping preparation.
Source evidence is also a first-class relationship. A product's nutrient profile, a package price, an ingredient conversion and a stock assertion each have their own provenance and effective basis. A recently opened page does not make any of them newly observed. The interface can keep these details collapsed, but calculations and explanations must be able to resolve them.
Five invariants users should be able to trust
Planning never records eating or consumes actual stock. Reservations are separate from on-hand quantities.
A purchase is recorded once, a batch consumes raw ingredients once, and eating a portion consumes prepared stock once.
Unknown values remain unknown. They do not become zero, a guessed quantity or an apparently complete green assessment.
Historical records use their pinned revisions. Editing a recipe or refreshing a price does not rewrite yesterday's meal or receipt.
A failed or repeated action cannot silently lose an edit or apply the same real-world event twice.
These invariants are source requirements. Their concrete interface manifestations and worked cases below are proposed design resolutions. They should appear in the acceptance suite and in the relevant screen specifications, rather than being confined to a technical appendix.
Plan changes have a review boundary
A draft plan is a proposal. Applying it changes future occurrences, reservations and derived grocery requirements together. The source requires expected-revision checks and all-or-nothing domain effects. A preview must therefore identify both what it is proposing and which source revisions it inspected.
Recommended preview contents are: added, changed and removed occurrences; preserved locks; affected leftover dependencies; newly required purchases; and unresolved constraints. Avoid presenting every unchanged meal as a decision. Show the material consequence near each proposed change and let the owner inspect details. A required exclusion cannot be softened through an innocuous-looking confirmation button.
When the owner applies a preview, revalidate affected inputs at the commit boundary. If another device changed a dependent plan, profile, recipe or stock fact, return an actionable conflict and preserve the proposal. Do not blindly apply old results. Conversely, an unrelated note edit should not invalidate the entire week's plan. The revision set should cover actual dependencies rather than use a global workspace timestamp for every action.
Recommended conflict action choices are “Review updated impact,” “Keep editing my proposal,” and “Discard this proposal.” Recompute against current evidence, identify what changed, and require confirmation for the revised material effects. A silent automatic rebase is unsuitable when it changes food restrictions, locked meals or purchase amounts. A harmless metadata reconciliation can remain automatic when no domain consequence changes.
The updated derived grocery requirement belongs to the same logical application result. If shopping is already active, preserve its current working fulfillment state and present a reviewable requirement diff rather than silently replacing its rows; applying that diff is a separate revision-checked shopping action. Existing manual items remain. Previously fulfilled quantities remain. Removing a planned curry may mark its unpurchased ingredients no longer needed, but it cannot erase a purchase already recorded or remove manually added soap.
Worked reservation example
Assume a synthetic pantry record contains 500 g of dry rice. The current plan reserves 300 g; a replacement draft needs 350 g for the same plan. During replacement, exclude that plan's superseded 300 g reservation before assessing the new requirement. The replacement fits, with 150 g available afterward. Counting both old and new reservations would invent a 150 g shortage. Reservations owned by other active plans remain relevant.
This case assumes compatible exact stock and no competing reservation. If the pantry only says “some rice,” the result is a pantry check, not a numerical claim that the plan fits. If another plan reserves 200 g before application, the 350 g replacement no longer fits without a revised purchasing plan; the preview must be refreshed.
Shopping records what happened in stages
The source distinguishes a checklist mark, purchase confirmation, stock assertion and consumption. A checkmark means picked up in the current shopping session. It does not establish a paid amount, add stock, or record intake. “Have some” is a qualitative stock assertion, not a purchase.
A binary checkbox alone cannot represent partial fulfillment. The recommended compact row keeps the quick check action but adds an expandable “Picked up” quantity control. For an exact requirement of two packs, choosing one pack shows “1 of 2 picked up” and leaves one outstanding. The full check action sets the known requested amount as picked up; when that amount is unresolved it opens the quantity entry instead of inventing it. This is a proposal requiring mockup and interaction review.
“Confirm purchases” presents the picked-up quantities, selected product/package identity, optional actual prices and any unresolved conversions. Confirming creates the purchase and acquisition effects as one domain operation. If the price is absent, stock can still be recorded while spend remains unknown. It must not become a zero-dollar purchase merely because a price field was skipped.
A successful confirmation marks the exact pending fulfillment records and quantities consumed by that confirmation without deleting their history. Recommended transaction rule: claim those unconfirmed fulfillment quantities under their expected revision in the same commit that creates the purchase and stock acquisition. Two devices submitting different operation IDs against the same pickup cannot both claim it; the second receives the already-confirmed result or an explicit changed-quantity conflict. A genuinely additional purchase must create a new pickup or acquisition record rather than reuse the consumed one. A repeated network request returns the same operation result. A second real purchase is a new operation, even if product, amount and date happen to match. Duplicate detection can ask for review; matching values alone cannot prove a duplicate.
Worked purchase and checkout example
Separate priced calculation example, retained from the preceding edition: a synthetic recipe plan requires 700 g of firm tofu. This is not the core visual trace’s 800 g requirement. Exact compatible on-hand stock is 100 g. The selected offer is 400 g per pack at USD 2.40. Missing quantity is 600 g; buying two packs provides 800 g for an estimated checkout of USD 4.80. The consumed portion allocation for 700 g is USD 4.20 using that offer basis, which is a different measure from the additional checkout.
The shopper picks up one pack. The list records 400 g picked up, with 200 g still to acquire in this shopping session. Actual stock remains 100 g until purchase confirmation; the 200 g shopping remainder is not an on-hand inventory claim. Confirming that pack adds 400 g to the previous 100 g of stock once. It records USD 2.40 actual spend only if that actual price is confirmed; otherwise it retains the offer as an estimate and actual spend as unknown. One more whole pack would satisfy the remaining 200 g under this package choice. Do not display a half-pack purchase unless the merchant actually sells that quantity.
If the plan later increases its requirement to 850 g, confirmed stock is 500 g before preparation. The new shortfall is 350 g. Keep the earlier acquisition and show the changed requirement. If the shopper checked the same row on two devices, desired state and stable operation identity prevent a double purchase; merely toggling a boolean twice does not represent this correctly.
The numbers above are synthetic arithmetic fixtures, not Matt's groceries, prices or current inventory.
Cooking and intake are different events
A cooking session records instructions being followed. Viewing the next step does not mark the previous one complete. Timer expiry does not establish that food is cooked. Exiting a session does not cancel real-world cooking, stop every timer, consume inventory or record a meal.
Finishing cooking offers two source-defined paths: record a batch with actual yield and ingredient adjustments, or finish without updating inventory. The latter is essential for users who want useful cooking guidance without detailed stock tracking. It should not leave the app in a broken or permanently nagging state.
Batch confirmation consumes raw ingredients and creates prepared output together. A leftover occurrence reserves that output; eating it creates a consumption event and reduces prepared stock. Canceling tomorrow's leftover lunch releases its reservation but does not claim the food was discarded. Waste is a separate explicit event or visibly labeled estimate.
Worked batch and correction example
A synthetic recipe revision has a planned yield of four portions and consumes 400 g of raw tofu. The cook confirms an actual batch yield of three portions using the same recorded ingredient quantities. Raw tofu decreases by 400 g once; the prepared batch begins with three portions. Its per-portion allocation derives from the actual batch basis, without silently changing the source recipe's four-portion yield. If four future portions were reserved, the confirmed three-portion yield exposes a one-portion shortfall. Recommend recording the real batch honestly while marking affected future allocations for repair; do not reject the actual observation merely to preserve a feasible plan. Existing reservation priority must be shown, with owner choices to reduce portions, replace one future meal, or record another preparation plan. Do not silently shrink all four meals or double-allocate three available portions. The batch confirmation and shortage status must become visible coherently. Before any affected future plan is treated as feasible, require the repair preview to be applied.
Recording one portion eaten reduces the batch to two portions. The raw tofu projection does not decrease again. Repeating the same intake operation after a lost response returns its original result. Undoing that consumption creates a reversal that restores one prepared portion once and corrects the nutrition projection.
Undoing the original batch after a portion has been consumed is no longer a simple safe reversal. Recommend a correction review showing the linked consumption and remaining stock. The source permits exact undo only when downstream effects can be reversed coherently; otherwise offer a correction with explicit consequences. Do not implement a generic “restore old workspace snapshot” button for this case.
An actual yield may be expressed in grams instead of servings. The design must retain its basis and any reviewed portion mapping; three containers do not automatically equal three nutritional servings. This remains a domain-specific quantity decision where the source lacks a confirmed mapping.
Nutrition scopes remain legible
The source requires ingredient-derived nutrition or an asserted recipe profile as the authority for a recipe, never both added together. Scaling uses compatible quantity, unit, preparation state and source revision. Dry and cooked rice are not interchangeable bases; a scoop or cup needs a product-specific mapping before conversion to mass.
The recommended analysis selector names the scope directly: selected meal, planned day, recorded so far, expected day, or selected week. Expected day combines recorded quantities with explicitly retained future or remaining planned quantities. It cannot add the full original plan on top of partial intake.
For example, a planned occurrence has two portions. The owner records half a portion. If they explicitly keep another half portion planned, expected intake is one portion total. If they close the occurrence at half a portion, expected intake is half a portion. Neither path silently assumes the original two portions will still be eaten.
Unknown nutrient contributors remain visible. With a synthetic lower-only target of 100 units and a known subtotal of 110 units, the modeled lower bound is met even if additional nonnegative contributions are unknown; the total is still incomplete. With an upper bound of 150 units, a subtotal of 110 plus unbounded unknown contributions cannot establish a pass. A known subtotal of 160 already exceeds that upper bound. These are arithmetic examples about configured values, not dietary or medical recommendations.
An average week view does not override daily bounds. Household recipe yield does not multiply one person's nutrition targets. Supplement schedules remain user-defined; optimization does not increase doses to make a target turn green.
Timers recover without inventing certainty
A running timer stores an absolute target timestamp and its revision. A paused timer stores remaining duration. The visible countdown is a projection of those facts, not the sole record. Navigation and reload reconstruct it. Multiple timers can run independently; pausing a cooking session does not necessarily pause them because food may still be heating.
Recommended controls keep Start, Pause, Resume and Add minute explicit. Reset requires a deliberate action. Adding a minute changes the target of a running timer or the remaining duration of a paused timer. Dismissing expiry acknowledges its display; it does not restart the timer or mark the step complete.
Clock changes are a real ambiguity. Use monotonic elapsed timing during an active session where available, and reconcile wall-clock/server observations on resume. If a large disagreement makes the remaining time unreliable, show “Check this timer” with the saved duration/start context and explicit correction controls. Do not promise precision across arbitrary device-clock changes. This wording is a proposed recovery design for a source-identified risk.
Background alerts depend on actual browser/platform capabilities and granted permissions. A beautiful timer screen cannot prove that an alert will fire while the device is asleep. The settings and active timer should make known limitations discoverable, without repeatedly interrupting routine cooking.
Offline actions preserve intent
The source minimum supports previously loaded groceries, manual additions, checklist changes, loaded recipe/session reading and local timer controls. It does not promise first-time uncached content, planning, external imports or image generation offline.
ACCEPTED BEHAVIOR
Accepted NQ-006 A (ND-016). Purchase, batch and intake drafts may be saved offline, clearly labeled Not yet recorded. Revalidate and review/confirm online before authoritative stock or intake effects. Reconnection must not silently auto-apply these actual-event drafts. The baseline offline minimum remains: loaded shopping lists and checklist/manual edits, loaded recipe/session reading and local timer controls. Broader provisional offline actual-event completion/autosync is unselected. Owner evidence: Sentinel_daee2f5ecd6081919e3a58e403a92023.

Private caches must be isolated by account/workspace. Offline sign-out or account switching cannot display another account's cached meals. Source images and signed media links require their own lifecycle; a cached URL is not a permanent public asset identity.
Transaction and external boundaries
The source requires all-or-nothing effects for profile activation, plan application, purchase plus stock, preparation plus batch, and intake correction plus batch adjustment. Import/restore also needs a coherent application boundary. Calling independent writes one after another does not satisfy that requirement.
External calendar and provider operations cannot generally share the same local transaction. Record durable intent and reconcile the external result using a stable operation key. After a timeout, check whether the event already exists before creating another. Calendar failure leaves nutrition tasks intact. Deleting a calendar block unschedules time; it does not delete a meal or record eating.
Expected revisions and operation identities solve different problems. Revision checks detect changes since review. An operation identity prevents the same logical command from applying twice. Both are needed. The same identity with a materially different payload should be rejected or reconciled explicitly, rather than repurposed silently; this is a recommended contract tightening.

Artwork providers and portable data
A useful app without a connected provider
Nooch's core experience must survive missing image generation, external nutrition lookup and calendar access. The source specification R17–19 and R24–25 makes ordinary photos, manual editing, prebuilt artwork, internal preparation tasks and readable exports first-class paths. This chapter develops their proposed controls and recovery behavior. It does not assert that any provider or integration currently works.
SOURCE REQUIREMENT
A page view, theme change, resized window or equipment toggle never triggers a paid generation job. Appearance is presentation state. Food rules, quantities, recipe identity and equipment capability must remain unchanged when artwork changes.

RECOMMENDED DESIGN
Show the current capability near the action that needs it. An unavailable scene-generation action offers “Use a photo” and “Keep simple layout,” plus a short explanation of the missing capability. Do not replace the entire meal page with a configuration error or make the owner visit technical settings before reading their recipe. A small Settings link can explain configuration separately.

Issue to prevent: a decorative scene can look more authoritative than the data behind it. Generated food art is serving inspiration, not evidence of ingredients, portions, allergens or nutritional completeness. Reject a scene that visibly introduces conflicting ingredients; do not change the recipe to match the image.
Three intentional presentation treatments
A composed scene places food in a curated environment with space for real interface elements. An editorial photo uses an ordinary user or licensed image with an intentional crop. A minimal treatment uses typography, useful meal information and restrained placeholders. All three must retain the same actions and truthful data hierarchy.
The source's initial immersive families are kitchen-table and overhead-tabletop, each with light/evening and wide/compact compositions. This describes eight empty template references, not eight finished meal images. Finished food compositions require their own production and review. Breakfast-counter and shared-table families are later possibilities, not mandatory first-release work.
Source-required selection order respects the user’s separate presentation choice: Minimal stays minimal; Editorial uses an eligible photo or Minimal; Immersive uses a compatible approved scene, then an explicitly approved compatible cutout, then ordinary photography, then Minimal. Appearance, presentation and generation permission remain separate settings. Never display an old incompatible dish simply to fill a large hero. A failed image request should replace only the media region, preserve controls and avoid a sudden layout jump.
DECISION NEEDED
NQ-007: how prominent should immersive scenes be in the first release?
A — A small curated set plus polished editorial/minimal fallback (recommended). This makes quality achievable while retaining a delightful visual identity. Many saved meals will use simpler presentation.
B — Require an immersive scene for every saved meal. This creates a consistent idealized gallery but adds generation/review cost, delays and a larger failure surface.

The recommendation follows the source's bounded asset approach. The exact selected artwork and release treatment still require review.

Export is useful even before full backup
The source distinguishes human-readable outputs from a semantically complete portable record. A recipe PDF, weekly menu or grocery list is a review/print view. CSV is a convenient tabular view. Neither is a complete restore package. JSON with exact quantities, revisions and references provides the domain representation, while an optional media bundle carries original binaries.
DECISION NEEDED
NQ-009: what should the default export contain?
A — Structured data with a clear media manifest, plus a separate opt-in full media archive (recommended). This keeps routine exports small and makes omissions explicit. A data-only export cannot restore missing private originals by itself.
B — Always include every supported original media file. This is a more self-contained backup but may be slow or large and needs transfer/storage limits.

Show the selected scope, record count, media count, estimated size where available, and omissions before export. Exclude secrets, access tokens, payment instruments and credential-bearing URLs. Calendar links remain non-authoritative external references; importing them does not grant access or establish a live connection. Restore does not regenerate missing artwork or incur generation cost without a separate explicitly authorized request. A checksum demonstrates byte integrity, not semantic completeness or authorization.
Readable print output should use real text and document structure, not just a screenshot. Recipe quantities must match the selected revision and scale. Use light print styling, readable repeated table headers and sensible pagination for both supported paper sizes. Preserve fractional values and long ingredient names. Import and restore are different commitments
Recommended import flow: choose file, validate and stage, preview matches/conflicts, choose duplicate handling, then apply. Staging has no live domain effects. Identify unsupported versions, missing references, invalid units, cyclic components, ownership remapping, oversized content and media omissions before application.
Duplicate options must be specific. Keeping an existing recipe leaves it untouched; copying creates a separate identity and remaps dependent references; accepting a new revision retains the history and must not rewrite pinned historical occurrences. “Replace everything” is not a harmless duplicate-resolution button.
Import application requires all-or-nothing domain effects and expected revision checks over affected records. If current records changed since preview, preserve staged work, rebuild the impact preview and obtain confirmation of the changed effects before applying. Failed application must leave no partially remapped live graph. External effects require durable reconciliation rather than being mislabeled part of a database transaction.
A full restore replaces only manifest-covered workspace data, never account identity, credentials, security policy or access grants. It needs a distinct recoverable checkpoint and confirmation flow. A verified recoverable checkpoint is a prerequisite: if it cannot be established, block destructive restore and offer non-destructive staged import or export instead. Explain which current records would change and validate the restore against the relevant workspace revision barrier. A failed restore retains the prior authoritative state. Give the operation a durable, idempotent identity and explicit completed/failed outcome; if interrupted, resume or reconcile that operation rather than start an ambiguous second restore. Preserve imported historical facts as history, while surfacing any conflicts with current rules in future plans instead of rewriting the past. Do not call a successful parse a successful restore.
After application, verify semantic round trips: quantities and units, source/revision links, plan occurrences, purchase/stock/intake relationships, selected artwork metadata and omissions. An export containing only today’s projection cannot recreate the full history behind it. No actual import or restore is authorized by producing this design document.
