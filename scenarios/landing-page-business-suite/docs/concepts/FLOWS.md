# Business account credit funding flows

## Funding and spend

`business_account_credit_transactions` remains the immutable grant ledger. Each accepted account grant creates one `credit_funding_lots` row in the same database transaction and links the transaction through `lot_id`. The external event key protects grant replay. Stripe event `livemode` is carried from the verified webhook envelope through normalized grant metadata into the lot; only an explicit true value is live-mode. New grants always store explicit live or test mode. Historical wallet balances and bonuses are not imported into lots and cannot fund reservations, direct usage, or settlement. A cached wallet balance alone is never funding authority.

A request first reserves against the account's spendable lot total minus all unexpired pending reservations. A reservation does not decrement a lot. Settlement locks spendable lots, allocates the measured charge by the stable priority order below, updates each lot's remaining credits, records one allocation per lot and operation, and writes the usage event atomically. The business wallet row retains account and billing identity; its historical amount columns are inert. `UsageReportRequest.operation_id` is carried into direct account usage events; an identical replay returns success without another usage increment or lot allocation, while conflicting replay data fails.

The displayed account wallet balance is also derived from attributed lots for the matching account and billing identity: open, settled, known-mode lots that have reached their spendable time and have not expired. Reservation authority further selects the configured live or test mode and subtracts pending holds. Historical values left in the wallet cache do not appear as available credits.

Spend priority: earliest expiry first; then lots outside their reversal window; then the earliest-ending reversal window; then oldest creation time and lot ID. Lots must be open, settled, past `spendable_after`, unexpired, have a known mode matching the route's runtime configuration. Unknown-mode, pending, frozen, failed, reversed, expired, and test-mode lots cannot fund a live route.

## State machines (SM1 / SM2)

SM1 is the lot status: `open -> frozen | exhausted | expired | reversed`; `frozen -> open | expired | reversed`; `exhausted`, `expired`, and `reversed` are terminal. A partial debit keeps `open`; a debit that consumes the remaining balance reaches `exhausted`. Signed Stripe events drive freezes, refunds and dispute corrections through `funding_reversal.go`; callers cannot choose these outcomes.

Provider settlement is a separate axis of SM1 with values `pending`, `settled`, `failed`, and `reversed`. This account-grant path inserts `settled` lots. A lot is spendable only when its status is `open` and its provider settlement is `settled`, subject to mode, time and reservation checks. Refund and dispute state is separate from lot exhaustion; see [funding reversal lifecycle](../internal/TEMPORAL-FLOWS.md#funding-reversal-lifecycle) for its contract and ledger ownership.

SM2 is the reservation lifecycle: `pending -> finalized | overrun | released | expired`. Settlement records measured `actual_amount`, caps customer `charged_amount` and funding-lot allocations at `reserved_amount`, and stores the excess in `overrun_amount`; provider `cost_micros` remains the observed provider cost. Positive overrun is terminal. Exact replays of finalized or overrun reservations succeed without another allocation; conflicting settlement metadata is rejected. Expiry and release remove the hold from spendable capacity only when dispatch has not made the outcome uncertain. Finalization writes allocations and the usage event in one transaction. Funding loss rollups and ledger deltas are owned by `funding_reversal.go` and `funding_ledger.go`.

The lifecycle topology is owned by `api/internal/commerce/fundinglot/flow/flow.json` and `api/internal/commerce/reservation/flow/flow.json`. Flow Verifier generates and checks their Quint models and production transition helpers through `make temporal-models`; the formal replay tests consume the same generated matrix. Commerce retains SQL ownership: each mutation writes the generated target status under an atomic predicate for its expected source status. Repository tests separately prove balances, source attribution, livemode, idempotency, and concurrent transactions; the abstract formal models do not prove those numeric or SQL properties. No source handler makes a policy decision about risk, margin, or account trust.

The composed repository model in `api/internal/commerce/composed_money_model_postgres_test.go` enumerates status, provider settlement, source attribution, mode, timing, value, and account binding against Postgres. It separately checks pending holds, measured usage versus capped charge, app and global caps, terminal replay, concurrent settlement, and refund/dispute ordering with ledger deltas. `api/journeytest/composed_money_door_test.go` checks unavailable-lot refusals and an exact settled replay through the mounted Door and Gateway. The generated finite product flow in `api/internal/commerce/moneycomposition/flow/flow.json` binds its selected no-effect refusal and settlement/replay traces to complete typed observations from those same Postgres-backed Door/Gateway checks. This proves the declared relation for those observations; the full SM1/SM2 interleaving target remains held.

## Safety boundaries

Personal-email credit grant, debit, balance fallback and reservation authority are retired. The historical `credit_reservations` table is retained only for explicit `count_based` quota, scoped to the authenticated principal with server-derived tier and the existing default/custom window. This quota owner rejects cost-based meters before persistence and never reads funding lots. Test funding requires explicit `livemode=false` at grant and is excluded from a live-mode usage service. Money Ledger owns postings; commerce emits immutable source-attributed deltas and retains uncertain delivery for retry.


## Meter kind, app scope, and admission hold

`limit_type` describes the meter (`cost_based` or `count_based`); `app_bundle_key` describes its scope. The exact legacy `app_specific/ai_credits` row is interpreted as money only with a nonempty app scope and valid positive conversion. Other ambiguous legacy rows, duplicate same-scope caps, and conflicting meter or conversion declarations fail closed. A credit operation must satisfy both its global cap and exact app cap when app-scoped. The dollar app-limit editor writes explicit `cost_based` plus app scope; count values remain counts.

Public device issuance creates a global account credential after membership verification; requested app metadata cannot grant billing authority. Global credentials can spend when no app cap requires a verified binding. Trusted app-bound credentials enforce both global and exact-app caps. The immutable key scope follows planning, reservation, dispatch, replay and settlement. `internal/aidoor/recovery.go` reconciles durable Gateway receipts after disconnect or process loss; an unknown provider outcome retains the hold. See [AI credit admission and settlement](../internal/TEMPORAL-FLOWS.md#ai-credit-admission-and-settlement).
