# Go To Market — Audio Tools

Audio Tools is primarily a shared capability, with owned hosted voice adding
subscription or credit value inside consuming applications. This is a product
direction and validation plan, not a priced offer or launch approval.

## Purpose Of This Document

Connect the voice experience, adoption path and commercial hypotheses to proof.
The PRD owns scope; MONETIZATION.md owns packaging proposals and policy decisions.

## Audience And Positioning

Users need responsive, trustworthy dictation without understanding provider
plumbing. Developers need one portable integration. Operators need visible local
capacity, BYOK choice and an owned-service option with understandable usage.

Positioning: use voice in the app you are already using, with an explicit
processing route and reliable recovery. Local/BYOK alternatives remain available;
owned hosting can remove local setup and key-management friction where qualified.
Do not market universal devices, unlimited use, guaranteed speed or a functioning
hosted route before matching evidence exists.

## Channels

| Channel | Hypothesis | Required proof |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Voice entrypoints in consuming scenarios | Useful dictation makes owned hosting valuable at the point of use | Paced consumer qualification and opt-in activation data |
| Shared adoption guide | Thin integrations spread a consistent capability across apps | Second-consumer and regression evidence |
| Existing subscription/credit catalog | One shared offer can fund hosted voice across apps | Approved pricing, live delivery and reconciled usage; no separate consumer ledger |

These are proposed channels, not authorization to publish marketing or contact users.

## Launch Motion

1. Qualify local shared-consumer STT as the first slice.
2. Prove BYOK and subscription/credit controls with isolated simulations.
3. Qualify actual hosted inference and billing under an approved live grant.
4. Qualify the named device/language matrix and operation-specific product paths.
5. Offer only the scope backed by current receipts and approved catalog policy.

A local release can precede the owned route; it cannot advertise complete
commercial voice. TTS and summarization need separate delivery and meter evidence.

## Messaging

| Message | Evidence required | State |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Responsive streamed dictation | Approved cohort SLOs and visible partial/final traces | Target, not established claim |
| Your choice of local, BYOK or owned processing | Actual-route, failure and consent tests | Full route qualification pending |
| Hosted voice works across adopting apps | Real delivery, consumer tests and reconciled shared usage | Not launch-qualified |
| Portable by design | Shared adapter conformance and named native-device receipts | Architecture target; scope-specific proof needed |

## Validation Experiments

Measure voice start-to-success conversion, route selection, recoverable failures,
time-to-first-use and owned-service activation using consented metadata. Keep
provider cost, customer debit and business revenue separate. Pricing,
willingness-to-pay, conversion and unit-economics thresholds need commercial-owner
review; no revenue or adoption result is asserted here.
