# Go To Market

> Strategy canon lives in `docs/monetization/strategy/STRATEGY.md` and the brand
> voice canon in `docs/marketing/STRATEGY.md`. This document records what is
> specific to Backdrop Studio.

## The wedge

**Distinctive page imagery is currently either expensive or generic.** A team
either commissions a designer or accepts the default — and the default has become
recognisable. Roughly three quarters of commercial pages launched in early 2026
carry a detectable generation fingerprint in their visual layer, the most
reliable being a blue-to-purple gradient hero. Polished has become cheap, and
cheap now reads as cheap.

Backdrop Studio sells the other side of that line: imagery that is legibly
*processed* — screened, quantized, palette-locked — at a cost closer to the
default than to a commission.

## Sequencing

The order matters, because each stage produces the evidence the next one needs.

1. **Dogfood.** Replace the `landing-page-business-suite` hero with a released
   backdrop. This is the first consumer integration and the first proof.
2. **Measure.** Conversion on the rebuilt landing page is the honest evidence for
   whether any of this claim holds. Report it whichever way it comes out.
3. **Portfolio.** Extend to the other public surfaces — sign-up screens,
   open-graph cards, scenario splash imagery. Marginal cost per surface should be
   near zero by this point; if it is not, the catalog is wrong.
4. **Bundle.** Ship in the business bundle and as a desktop app.
5. **Packs.** Curated style packs as a distinct offer, once the catalog has
   demonstrated it holds together as a system.

Stage 2 is a real gate, not a formality. If distinctive imagery does not move
conversion on our own page, that finding should change the roadmap rather than
be quietly dropped.

## Positioning

**What it is:** a studio for the imagery that sits *behind* the message.

**What it is not:** an image generator, a page builder, or a general-purpose
editor. Being narrow is the point — the taxonomy, the placement model, and the
legibility gate are only coherent because the scope is ambient imagery for
layouts.

**The line that carries it:** *the treatment is the brand.* Because every lane
terminates in the same deterministic pass, output is palette-locked and
consistent whether it came from code or a model. That is a systems claim rather
than an aesthetic one, and it is defensible.

## Proof assets

Ordered by how much they convince:

1. **Our own landing page**, rebuilt, with conversion numbers attached.
2. **A contact sheet** — one subject across ten treatments, one treatment across
   four subjects. The specimen sheet is the demo; it shows the system rather than
   describing it.
3. **A remix lineage** — one style forked across an axis, with the family
   visible. Demonstrates the catalog compounds.
4. **A legibility refusal** — a beautiful image the gate rejects, and the scrim
   that fixes it. This is the credibility asset: it shows the product knows the
   difference between pretty and usable, which nothing else in the category does.

## Risks

- **The category reads as a toy.** Mitigated by leading with the legibility gate
  and the reproducibility story rather than with pretty pictures.
- **Taste is not transferable.** A catalog of fifteen strong styles beats forty
  thin ones, and which lineages Vrooli should speak in is an art-direction call
  that no amount of engineering answers.
- **The trend is a trend.** Halftone and dither are current. The five-axis model
  and the treatment engine outlive any particular lineage, which is why the
  taxonomy is the asset and the seed catalog is not.
