## Reserved-copy legibility

The catalog's headlines are legible where the catalog says they go: **20 of 23
measured styles**, ratios 8.57 to 19.82 against a 4.50 bar, measured through a
really running `image-tools`. `make integration-evidence` reproduces the table
in [`../evidence/legibility/reserved-copy.md`](../evidence/legibility/reserved-copy.md).

Three mechanisms, in the order they mattered:

1. **The generator opens a clearing.** `scenes.QuietZone` lifts the declared
   area to the top of the tonal ramp before any treatment runs, so the picture
   composes around its copy instead of the copy being dropped onto whatever the
   picture happened to draw. Every one of the thirteen raster generators is
   pinned to clear its worst pixel there.
2. **The chain honours it.** A clearing alone is not enough — a screen puts ink
   and paper everywhere it runs and will print over a clearing as readily as
   over anything else. `Knockout` (D-020) reserves the area on every colour
   operation, applied before each one so the operation returns its own paper
   rather than a hole.
3. **The ruler was wrong.** `legibility.cropBounds` searched from the top of the
   frame rather than from the top of the region, so completed repairs reported
   as failures. This is recorded in full in [`PROBLEMS.md`](PROBLEMS.md), and it
   is the reason the first two mechanisms looked ineffective when they were not.

4. **The vector lane cuts its own** (D-021). A vector style declares no
   treatments, so there is no operation for a knockout to attach to. The
   generator emits the reserve itself, in both polarities — a knockout in the
   paper ink for dark copy, a solid in the dark ink for light copy — as a
   VOLUME extended by the frontmost plate's travel, on every plane. Both of
   those qualifications were bought by measurement: cut as a rectangle on the
   front plate alone, a reserve is legible at rest and gone one scroll later.

The remaining three are model-backed styles with light copy. The treatment
reserve serves dark copy only, and D-022 records why: a solid large enough to
give light copy dark ground costs more subject survival than the perceptual gate
allows, so the reserve gives way rather than the quality floor. Their answer is
compositional and belongs with the model lane's plate work.

Three treatments changed to make a reserve mean anything, and two were carrying
defects of their own. `stipple` deposited one full-strength pixel per cell at
any tone, so every stippled highlight in the catalog carried ink the tone never
called for; `ascii_mosaic` stamped a whole glyph inward from a cell straddling
the edge; `displacement` fetches from elsewhere and now damps its own warp
inside a reserve.
