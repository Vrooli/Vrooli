# Decisions — Offer Desk

This document records durable decisions and tradeoffs future agents
should not accidentally relitigate.

## Purpose Of This Document

Use this document when a choice:

- affects multiple files or future agents,
- rejects a plausible alternative,
- changes architecture, deployment, data, security, monetization, or
  testing direction,
- needs a revisit trigger.

Routine implementation log entries belong in [`PROGRESS.md`](PROGRESS.md).
Known unresolved issues belong in [`PROBLEMS.md`](PROBLEMS.md).

## Decision Log

| Date | Decision | Context | Consequences | Revisit Trigger |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2026-08-13 | Use the generated `react-vite` scenario documentation contract. | Scenario scaffold was generated from the template. | Docs start with stubs and maturity metadata in `docs/manifest.json`. | Revisit when scenario adopts a different template or doc contract. |
| 2026-08-13 | A **delivery tier is a `variant` node**, not a sixth node kind. | The source canon holds tiers in `strategy/TIERS.md` with their own four-state lifecycle (`active` / `candidate` / `north-star` / `retired`), declared *orthogonal* to the SKU catalog. The node kinds here are `offer`, `variant`, `channel`, `revenue-line`, `deliverable`; `tier` appears in none of them. | An offer is *sold at* a variant, so "Business Bundle at Tier 2" is one offer-to-variant edge and pricing is a property of that edge — which is exactly the orthogonality the source document asserts. Tiers therefore inherit the one shared status vocabulary instead of keeping a private one. The `north-star` state does not survive the move: it maps to `idea`, whose semantics ("captured, not planned against") are identical. | A second axis needs the same orthogonal treatment (e.g. geography or currency zone) and edge properties stop being expressive enough. |
| 2026-08-13 | An **add-on is an `offer` with a `requires` edge to its parent**, not a distinct node kind. | `catalogs/CATALOG.md` splits SKUs into base bundles and add-ons, and asserts membership must be many-to-many. | Guardrail 3 ("no add-on activation before parent bundle has paying users") becomes a transition precondition on the `requires` edge rather than prose — an add-on promotion is refused while its parent has no paying-user fact. Many-to-many survives because an offer may carry several `requires` edges. | An add-on needs pricing or entitlement semantics that differ structurally from an offer, which would mean commerce concerns have leaked in and belong upstream. |
| 2026-08-13 | **No feature candidates are recorded for this scenario**, and no standalone competitive positioning is pursued. | A 2026-08-13 competitive scan found no product joining a lifecycle-enforced catalog to a ledger of actuals — which strengthens the Money Ledger pairing but does not establish standalone demand. An unoccupied space beside mature adjacent markets is more often unwanted than unnoticed. | The roadmap is whatever makes Money Ledger's surfaces better, in the order the PRD sequences. Absence of a competitor is explicitly not treated as demand evidence; the revisit trigger still requires operators to describe the pain unprompted. | The `../business/MONETIZATION.md` revisit trigger fires — all three parts. |
| 2026-08-13 | **The funnel is not modelled here.** | `strategy/FUNNEL.md` describes stage conversion metrics. | Funnel stages are measurements over channels and revenue lines, not records with a lifecycle. Modelling them would put a metrics pipeline inside a catalog. Channels remain nodes; their conversion numbers stay in the ledger and telemetry. | Channel attribution telemetry ships and a funnel stage acquires a state an operator must disposition. |
| 2026-08-13 | **This scenario is the `monetization` team's instrument**; Money Ledger is covered by it, not a second address. | `path:docs/agent-system/TARGET_MODEL.md` §5 defines an instrument as the one scenario a team reads to answer "what is the state of the world I own, and what should I do next". The team's `instrument` block declares `status: none` with archetype `production-ledger` and a gap marker naming `docs/monetization/catalogs/` — the catalog, not the financial model. Four of the team's five members work catalog-side; one is financial. | The declaration becomes `{ scenario: offer-desk, coversScenarios: [offer-desk, money-ledger] }`, matching the one live precedent in the fleet (`meta-optimization` → `meta-optimization-manager`). The `board` domain is the single address and must therefore carry financial-posture rows as well as offer rows (see the next row but one). Money Ledger stays the *marketed* surface and the operator's daily tool — instrument is a work-queue role, not a statement of product importance, and conflating the two is the likeliest misreading of this decision. | The member mix shifts to majority-financial, or `board` is promoted out of this scenario (see the next row). |
| 2026-08-13 | Hosting the `board` **inside the scenario that owns the catalog is accepted**, against a precedent that went the other way. | `TARGET_MODEL.md` §5 invariant 2 says an instrument never authors the denominators it measures against, and deviation `D6` names the failure. The `director-swarm` team applied exactly this rule to disqualify `swarm-manager` from grading the portfolio it holds, choosing `command-center` because it "owns none of what it would observe". This scenario owns the offer graph and would rank it. | Judged acceptable here for two reasons that do not hold in the `director-swarm` case. **First, the circularity is already broken structurally**: two of the board's three queue sources are external to this scenario — actuals come from Money Ledger, and trigger facts are operator-supplied (or, at `OT-P2-004`, another scenario's) — and `OT-P0-005` makes promotion to `active` an operator-role call, so an agent cannot author a success criterion and then satisfy it. **Second, there is no third candidate**: `director-swarm` had a scenario owning none of what it observed; monetization does not, and the `board` domain already owns nothing and derives every entry at read time, which is the property a separate scenario would have been built to obtain. Recording this so a later audit reads a decision rather than filing a `D6`. | An agent gains the ability to both author a trigger and satisfy it without an operator action — at which point promote `board` to its own scenario rather than weakening `OT-P0-005`. |
| 2026-08-13 | **The board carries financial posture, not only offer actuals.** | If the team has one address, the `financial-tracker` member must be able to read runway, goal verdicts and the default-alive gap from it. `OT-P1-002` as written covers only the per-offer earned-versus-intended join. | `board` gains a second Money-Ledger-sourced entry class — runway, goal verdicts with sustain-window progress, and the default-alive gap — surfaced under the same never-zero, named-availability discipline as the actuals join. These are **surfaced, never owned**: no amount is stored here, which keeps the PRD non-goal ("no accounting or balances") intact. Expressed as requirement `INT-005` under `OT-P1-003` rather than as a new operational target, because it widens the ranked board's scope rather than adding a capability. Each member declares the direct `money-ledger` call as its fallback, per the §5 degradation contract. | The posture rows outgrow ranking and start needing their own query surface, which would mean a reporting concern has leaked in. |
| 2026-08-13 | **Price ownership is split three ways** and stated here because three documents disagreed. | This scenario's PRD non-goals say "No pricing or billing"; the delivery-tier decision above says pricing is a property of the offer-to-variant edge; and `strategy/PRICING.md` holds a matrix that is exactly SKU × tier — that is, offer × variant. | **Intended** price is a property of the offer-to-variant edge and lives here, because an intended price is intent and intent is this scenario's entire boundary. **Charged** price lives in the commerce scenario upstream. **Received** amount lives in Money Ledger. The PRD non-goal is read as excluding billing and payment collection, not as excluding the intended figure on an edge. | An intended price acquires billing semantics — proration, tax, currency conversion — at which point it has stopped being intent and belongs upstream. |
| 2026-08-13 | **Benchmark and market-scan records move to the `gates` fact registry.** | `market-validator` maintains dated competitor comps with per-dimension staleness thresholds (pricing 90d, retention/activation 180d, channel-cac 120d, other 365d) and a sweep that enqueues anything past its threshold. Those are records with a status and a rule nothing can refuse — `D3` and `D4` — and neither this scenario nor Money Ledger claimed them. | A comp becomes a fact with an observation date and a dimension-derived staleness window. This reuses machinery `gates` already needs: staleness is a property of a fact, and a stale comp is precisely the *unknown, not false* case `GATE-003` already specifies. `market-validator` then reads the same one address as every other member instead of maintaining a private surface. The `benchmark-staleness-sweep` skill collapses to a thin pointer once the window is evaluated on schedule. | The fact registry starts needing comp-specific structure — source hierarchies, applicability weighting — that no trigger consumes, which would mean benchmarks want their own owner after all. |
| 2026-08-13 | The **gated-lifecycle engine is a general capability**, and is deliberately not being pursued as one yet. | The capability actually being built is: declare a condition, evaluate it on a schedule, refuse illegal transitions, require an operator for the promoting one, and treat an unknown fact as unknown rather than false. Offers are one instance of that shape; the same engine would serve any domain where records wait on conditions nobody remembers to re-check. | Recorded so the generalisation is neither missed nor performed by accident. The scenario stays offer-shaped: node kinds, the status vocabulary, and the board are all offer vocabulary, and generalising them now would cost the concreteness that makes the first version buildable. `../business/MONETIZATION.md` defers standalone packaging on the grounds that most operators lack enough *offers* to need a graph — that argument is about offers and does not transfer to the engine. | A second domain inside the fleet wants declared-condition-plus-scheduled-evaluation, at which point extract the engine rather than copying `gates`. |
